Rubin Harold Landau (born 1944) is a Professor Emeritus of Oregon State University's physics faculty, and a Fellow of the American Physical Society. 

 Early life and education 
Rubin Harold Landau, the son of Philip and Bertha (Israel) Landau, was born in 1944 in New York City. He married Rebecca L. Kamelhar in 1965, and they had a daughter, Loretta. They divorced in 1984, and Landau married Janet Wilson Harris in 1986.

Landau earned his baccalaureate degree at Cornell University in 1965. His 1966 Master of Science, as well as his 1970 Doctor of Philosophy, were both from the University of Illinois. His dissertation, titled Models for meson-deuteron scattering in the resonant region, was advised by D.G. Ravenhall.

 Career 
Landau held research associate positions at the University of Pittsburgh (1970–1972) and the University of British Columbia (1972–1974) before accepting a professorship at Oregon State University in 1974. Landau held joint appointments there in Physics and Computer Science Departments from 1996 to 2005. 

From 1974–1984 he was a visiting faculty member at the TRIUMF in Vancouver, Canada; and in 1977 at Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory. In 1980 he was a senior fellow at the University of Surrey in Guildford, England. Landau founded OSU's Computational Physics degree program, and has written more than 100 papers describing "computational studies of the scattering of elementary particles from subatomic systems," as well as "momentum space quantum mechanics."

 Awards, honors
