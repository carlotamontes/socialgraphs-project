James Andrew McCammon (born 1947, Lafayette, Indiana, US) is an American physical chemist known for his application of principles and methods from theoretical and computational chemistry to biological systems. He was Distinguished Research Professor of Chemistry and of Pharmacology at the University of California, San Diego, McCammon's research focused on the theoretical aspects of biomolecular and cellular activity. In 2011 he was elected to the National Academy of Sciences.

Prof. McCammon co-authored Dynamics of Proteins and Nucleic Acids (Cambridge University Press, 1987; ), an important contribution to molecular mechanics and molecular dynamics, with Stephen Harvey, while the first published reports on molecular mechanics and molecular dynamics can be found as early as 1976, most likely even earlier.

Early life
McCammon was born in Lafayette, Indiana and attended Pomona College, graduating in 1969 with a B.A. in chemistry. He received his M.A. and Ph.D. from Harvard University. At Harvard he worked with John Deutch on the biological applications of statistical mechanics and theoretical chemist Martin Karplus.

Career
In the 1980s he helped establish computer-aided drug design at Agouron Pharmaceuticals (acquired by Pfizer), contributing to the development of nelfinavir.
He received the Cray Research Information Technology Leadership Award for Breakthrough Computational Science "for his pioneering use of supercomputing technology in analyzing chemical enzyme inhibitors to fight Alzheimers and other diseases."
