Detlef Günther (born 21 November 1963) is a German-Swiss analytical chemist and professor at ETH Zurich. He has worked in analytical chemistry, particularly mass spectrometry, laser ablation, and trace-element analysis.

Günther served as Vice President Research and Corporate Relations at ETH Zurich from 2015 to 2020 and as Vice President Research from 2021 to 2022.

He is a member of the German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina and a Fellow of the Royal Society of Chemistry.

 Early life and education 

Günther was born on 21 November 1963. He attended primary school in Lübs from 1970 to 1978. He attended the Gymnasium in Zerbst, completing his secondary education in 1982. He studied chemistry at Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg, graduating as a Diplomchemiker in 1987.

He received the degree of Dr. rer. nat. from the same university in 1990.

 Career 

From 1992 to 1994, Günther worked in the Department of Stress Research at the Institute of Plant Biochemistry in Halle in the group of L. Nover.

In 1994, he moved to the Department of E arth Sciences at Memorial University of Newfoundland, where he worked as a postdoctoral researcher with H. P. Longerich.

From 1995 to 1998, Günther worked in the Department of Isotope Geology and Mineral Resources at ETH Zurich with C. A. Heinrich. During this period, he worked on laser-ablation ICP-MS and its application to geological materials, including fluid inclusions.

Günther joined ETH Zurich as an assistant professor of analytical chemistry and trace-element analysis in 1998. He became an associate professor and later a full professor. He has headed the Research Group for Elemental and Trace Analysis at ETH Zurich.

From 1999 to 2003, he chaired the Center of Excellence in Analytical Chemistry at ETH Zurich. He was Head of the Laboratory of Inorganic Chemistry from 2006 to 2008 and chaired the Department of Chemistry and Applied Biosciences from 2010 to 2012.

From 2015 to 2020, Günther served as Vice President Research and Corporate Relations at ETH Zurich. He served as Vice President Research from 2021 to 2022.

 Research 

Günther has contributed to the development of laser-ablation ICP-MS, including instrumentation for quantitative analysis of geological materials. In 1997, he and colleagues reported the use of a 193 nm argon-fluoride excimer laser for laser-ablation ICP-MS microanalysis.

His group has investigated aerosol formation and transport during laser ablation, including the effects of carrier gases and particle size on elemental fractionation in LA-ICP-MS. A 2013 study reported chemical imaging at high spatial resolution using the technique.

In 2012, Günther and colleagues reported a portable laser-ablation sampling device designed for elemental analysis of objects outside the laboratory. The system collected laser-generated aerosols for laboratory analysis and was developed for applications involving objects that could not readily be transported to a laboratory.

Günther's group has also worked on inductively coupled plasma time-of-flight mass spectrometry (ICP-TOFMS), including applications involving rapidly changing samples and single particles. Research from the group examined the use of ICP-TOFMS for the detection of nanoparticles in microdroplets.

In 2013, the group reported a prototype ICP-TOF mass spectrometer designed for temporally resolved, multi-element detection of short signals generated by individual particles and droplets.

In 2018, Günther and colleagues reported a nitrogen microwave-induced plasma system as an alternative to argon-based inductively coupled plasma for mass spectrometry.
