Staffan Einar Ström (14 September 1934 – 28 August 2016) was a Swedish physicist and engineer who was a professor at KTH Royal Institute of Technology. He is best known for his contributions to the null field method for electromagnetic, acoustic and elastodynamic problems.

Life and career
Born on 14 September 1934 in Gothenburg, Ström received his undergraduate degree in electrical engineering from Chalmers University of Technology in 1959. He obtained his doctoral degree in mathematical physics in 1967 from the same institution under the supervision of Nils Svartholm. From 1969 to 1971, he served as a visiting research associate at University of Texas at Austin under the supervision of E. C. George Sudarshan, working on group theory. Following his return to Chalmers University of Technology, he formed the Applied Mathematical Physics research group, focusing on applied mathematics in elastodynamics, electrodynamics and acoustics. In 1984, he was appointed the Chair of Electromagnetic Theory at KTH Royal Institute of Technology, where he worked at until his retirement in 1999. Following his retirement, he has continued his research activities, leading research projects in antenna applications from 2000 to 2004. 

Ström's early work focused on space-time transformation groups, such as Lorentz an Poincaré groups. His later work focused on the analysis of wave scattering: in the 1970s, he has made contributions to the null field method of Peter C. Waterman, extending it to more complex
scattering geometries as well as an ensemble of scatterers. He has also worked on embedding algorithms for inverse scattering problems and coauthored the monograph, Time Domain Wave-Splitting and Inverse Problems. 

Ström served as the chair of the  Swedish National Committee of Radio Science under the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences from 1994 to 2005. He was an active member of International Union of Radio Science, organizing the Electromagnetic Field Symposium in Stockholm in 1989 and chairing its Commission B from 1999 to 2002. In 2002, he was elected as a fellow of the IEEE "for contributions to null field approach to multiple scattering problems." He died on 28 August 2016, and was survived by his wife, their two daughters and his three siblings.
