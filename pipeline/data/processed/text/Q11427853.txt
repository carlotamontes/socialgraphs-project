Kazunari Dōmen (born September 24, 1953) is a Japanese chemist and materials scientist specializing in heterogeneous catalysis, photocatalysis, and solar-driven water splitting. He is a University Professor in the Office of University Professors at the University of Tokyo and a Distinguished University Professor at the Institute for Aqua Regeneration, Shinshu University.

 Early life and education 
Domen was born in 1953 in Japan. He studied at the University of Tokyo, receiving a bachelor's degree in 1976, a master's degree from its School of Science in 1979, and a Ph.D. in chemistry in 1982 with his thesis Photocatalytic Reaction on Some Semiconductor Powders.

After completing his doctorate, Domen joined the Chemical Resources Laboratory at Tokyo Institute of Technology as an assistant professor in 1982. He subsequently became an associate professor in 1990 and professor in 1996. During this period, he spent time as a visiting scientist at the IBM Almaden Research Center in California. 

 Career 
In 2004, Domen was appointed assistant professor at the Tokyo Institute of Technology. He stayed as a postdoctoral fellow at the IBM Almaden Research Center in San José, California in 1985/86. He was promoted to associate professor at the Tokyo Institute of Technology in 1990 and received a full professorship in 1996. Dōmen returned to his alma mater in 2004 as a professor. Together with Takashi Hisatomi, he has also been running a research laboratory at Shinshū University since 2017.

He began a cross-appointment with Shinshu University in 2017 and subsequently held positions at the university's Center for Energy & Environmental Science and Research Initiative for Supra-Materials. He became a Distinguished University Professor at Shinshu University's Institute for Aqua Regeneration in 2025.

Domen has also been involved in the organization of scientific conferences. In 2018, he served as chairperson of TOCAT8, the eighth Tokyo Conference on Advanced Catalytic Science and Technology, held in Yokohama. 

He has served as a reviewer for journals including Nature Materials, Nature Catalysis, Nature Energy, Nature Communications, Energy & Environmental Science, Chemical Science, Joule, Journal of the American Chemical Society, Angewandte Chemie International Edition, and ACS Catalysis.

 Research 
Domen and his collaborators developed particulate photocatalytic systems intended to make solar hydrogen production scalable. In 2016, his group reported particulate photocatalyst sheets capable of producing hydrogen and oxygen with a solar-to-hydrogen energy conversion efficiency exceeding 1 percent. Subsequent research addressed the durability and efficiency of photocatalysts and the design of reaction systems for large-scale deployment.

His group also developed a 100-square-meter solar hydrogen production system using particulate photocatalysts. The work demonstrated the possibility of scaling photocatalytic water splitting beyond laboratory-scale experiments and became an important component of Domen's research into practical solar hydrogen production.

In more recent research, Domen has focused on the materials and systems required for large-scale solar hydrogen production, including photocatalysts with higher solar-to-hydrogen conversion efficiencies, improved durability, reactor design, and gas separation. A 2025 review in Nature Reviews Materials examined materials and systems for large-scale photocatalytic water splitting, including work associated with Domen's research group.

In 2025, Domen published a major review on particulate photocatalysts for large-scale green hydrogen production. The article examined semiconductor photocatalysts, cocatalysts, particulate systems, visible-light response, panel reactors, and hydrogen and oxygen separation. He was a co-author of a Nature paper published in August 2026 reporting photocatalytic water splitting using a two-dimensional polymer with out-of-plane carrier flow.
