Jenő Attila Kessler (born 23 December 1939), commonly known as Eugen Kessler, is a Romanian biologist, palaeo-ornithologist, non-fiction author and academic. His research focuses on the fossil bird fauna of Romania and the Carpathian Basin, as well as the origin and evolution of birds.

 Biography 

Kessler worked as a porcelain painter in a porcelain factory from 1955 to 1958. He subsequently completed his secondary education at the Brassai Sámuel Theoretical Lyceum in Cluj-Napoca. He studied biology and geology at the Babeș-Bolyai University, graduating in 1963. Between 1963 and 1965, he worked as a teacher in Felsőrákos. From 1965 to 1970, he taught at the Faculty of Medicine in Oradea, followed by a teaching position at Primary School No. 13 until 1990. From 1990 to 1991, he taught at the Ady Endre Lyceum.

In 1991, Kessler became an assistant professor at the Faculty of Biology and Geology of Babeș-Bolyai University. He was promoted to associate professor in 1994 and professor in 1998. His teaching subjects included vertebrate zoology, comparative anatomy, autopsy, functional anatomy and biomechanics, ornithology, and ethology. He retired in 2002 and settled in Hungary. Subsequently, he became a lecturer at the Institute of Palaeontology of Eötvös Loránd University, where he taught courses in applied anatomy, dinosaurs and prehistoric birds.

Kessler began his research in 1969 under the supervision of palaeontologist Tibor Jurcsák, working as an external collaborator at the Natural History Department of the Țării Crișurilor Museum in Oradea. In 1977, he received his doctorate from the Faculty of Biology of the University of Bucharest. His research interests include the Mesozoic, Tertiary and Quaternary avifauna of Romania and the Carpathian Basin, as well as the origin and evolution of birds.

He has published his findings in scientific and educational articles and presented them at numerous conferences. Among his most significant contributions was the description of three bird taxa from the Early Cretaceous deposits of a bauxite mine near Cornet, close to Oradea. He has also studied fossil bird bones from sites in Romania, Hungary, the Republic of Moldova, Turkey, Slovakia and Serbia.
