Biography
Soai studied at the University of Tokyo, where he received his Ph.D. in 1979 in organic synthesis under Teruaki Mukaiyama and was a fellow of the Japan Society for the Promotion of Science. He conducted his postdoctoral studies with Ernest L. Eliel at the University of North Carolina. In 1981, he became a lecturer at Tokyo University of Science, and was promoted to associate professor (1986) and full professor (1991). In 2026, he was  jointly awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry  with Henri B. Kagan  "for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis."
 

He is involved in asymmetric and enantioselective synthesis, asymmetric autocatalysis, and the origin of chirality. In 1995, Soai discovered a reaction with the chiral compound 5-pyrimidyl alkanol that produced almost exclusively one of the two mirror-image forms of a molecule as a product, mimicking a process found in living organisms. In 2003, he was able to demonstrate this reaction with nonchiral reactants. Specifically, the Soai reaction for alkylation of pyrimidine-5-carbaldehyde with diisopropylzinc is named after him. In this reaction, both reactants, which are non-chiral, produce the chiral product pyrimidyl alcohol, which further induces that same chirality in subsequent catalytic cycles.

Soai was a visiting professor at several universities such as the ESPCI Paris (2001), Kyushu University (2005), Waseda University (2007–2010), University of Strasbourg (2008), and Jilin University (2010–2015). He retired as a professor in 2016.

In its 2026 Nobel announcement, the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences highlighted Soai's progression from his 1995 work on asymmetric autocatalysis to a 2003 reaction producing one of two possible mirror-image forms. It placed these results in the study of homochirality and the design of pharmaceutical synthesis. The prize money was shared equally with Henri B. Kagan.
