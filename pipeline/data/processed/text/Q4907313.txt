Bikas Kanta Chakrabarti (born 14 December 1952 in Kolkata (erstwhile Calcutta) is an Indian physicist.  At present he is Visiting Professor of Economics at the Indian Statistical Institute,  Kolkata, India.

Biography

Starting early 1900, the Institutions of Kolkata had many distinguished intellectual stalwarts of modern India, including physicists like Jagadish Chandra Bose, C. V. Raman, Satyendra Nath Bose, Meghnad Saha and Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis. During Chakrabarti's University days there were inspiring teachers and scientists like Amal Kumar Raychaudhuri (best known for Raychaudhuri equation) and Chanchal Kumar Majumdar (best known for Majumdar–Ghosh model).  

Chakrabarti received his Ph.D. degree from Calcutta University in 1979 (for research in Statistical Physics). Following post-doctoral work at the University of Oxford and the University of Cologne, he joined the faculty of Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics (SINP) in 1983. He is S. S. Bhatnagar Prize awardee (1997) and former J. C. Bose National Fellow (2011-2020). He is a former director of SINP.  At present he is Honorary  Visiting Professor of economics (2007-) at the Indian Statistical Institute. Emeritus Professor of SINP and of S.N. Bose National Centre for Basic Sciences. He was INSA Scientist (physics) at SINP (2021-2025).  

Much of Chakrabarti's research has centered around statistical  condensed matter physics (including Quantum annealing; see also Timeline of quantum computing) and applications to social sciences (see e.g., Econophysics). On these and related topics, he coauthored four reviews in  Reviews of Modern Physics, three books from Cambridge University Press and two books from Oxford University Press (mostly with his Ph. D. students). 

He was Ph. D. thesis supervisor of Anirban Chakraborti.
