John Courtney "Jack" Decius (1920 – 1994) was an American physical chemist, molecular spectroscopist, and researcher on the faculty of Oregon State College.  He made significant contributions to the study of vibrational spectra, dipole and ion interactions, and infrared and Raman spectroscopy of crystals and ionic species. 

 Early life and education 
John C. Decius was born Feb. 13, 1920, in San Francisco, the son of Lewis Courtney Decius and Grace (Towne) Decius. In 1941 he took his baccalaureate degree in chemistry at Stanford, and in 1947 earned his Ph.D. at Harvard, with a dissertation titled, I. Vibrational Potential Functions for Several Halomethanes and Halo-Ethanes II. The Principles of Dimensional Analysis and Scaled Models, advised by Edgar Bright Wilson.

Decius married Anne Streeter in 1948, and they raised a son, Peter S. Decius, and daughters Joyce S. Decius Lowry and Sylvia Russell. They had three grandchildren.

Decius died in 1994, due to injuries sustained in an auto accident.

 Career 
Decius became a professor of chemistry at Oregon State College in 1949. He retired as a designated "Distinguished Professor" in 1981.

 Awards and honors 
 1955 – Guggenheim Fellow
 1955-56 – Fulbright Fellow
 1956 – Sloan Fellow
 1962 – Fellow of the American Physical Society
