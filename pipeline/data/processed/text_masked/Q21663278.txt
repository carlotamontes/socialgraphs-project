Naomi McClure-Griffiths  (born July 11, 1975) is an American-born Australian astrophysicist and radio astronomer.
PRON is the first Chief Scientist of the Square Kilometre Array Observatory.
In 2004, McClure-Griffiths discovered a new spiral arm in the Milky Way galaxy.
PRON was awarded the Prime Minister's NAME Prize for Physical Scientist in 2006 and in 2015 was honored for PRON research in physics by receipt of the Pawsey Medal from the Australian Academy of Science.
This was followed by an Australian Laureate Fellowship in 2021, while in 2022 PRON was elected a Fellow of the Australian Academy of Science.
Early life and education NAME was born on July 11, 1975, in Atlanta Georgia.
PRON entered Oberlin College in 1993 where PRON studied both French and physics and then in 1997 entered the University of Minnesota to study astrophysics.
During PRON PhD, PRON participated in the International Galactic Plane Survey, leading the Southern Galactic Plane Survey to map the hydrogen gas in the Milky Way.
Career In 2001, PRON relocated permanently to Australia taking up a post doctoral fellowship at the Australia Telescope National Facility as a CSIRO Bolton Fellow.
During PRON fellowship, McClure-Griffiths studied the movement of interstellar gases and how explosions of stars create bubbles or shells which push the gasses out of the galaxy.
In their movement, chimneys of empty space may be created, two of which were discovered by McClure-Griffiths.
One of the chimneys PRON discovered is the only known chimney to "extend through the top and bottom of the galactic plane".
Then in 2004, PRON discovered a new spiral arm during PRON senior postdoctoral position.
The new arm was shown on previous mappings but never identified nor given a name.
McCure-Griffiths created a computer model to confirm its existence which was confirmed by PRON team.
In 2006, Naomi McClure-Griffiths was honored with the NAME Prize for Physical Scientist of the year one of the annual prizes awarded as the Prime Minister's Prizes for Science.
As Principal Investigator PRON initiated the Galactic All Sky Survey that same year and then in 2007, PRON was the recipient of the Powerhouse NAME from the Powerhouse Museum at the Sydney Observatory.
McClure-Griffiths' team took part in the international effort to complete the mapping of the Milky Way's magnetic fields in 2011.
In 2015, PRON left CSIRO and joined the Australian National University as a professor conducting PRON research from the Mount Stromlo Observatory.
That same year, PRON work in physics was recognized by receipt of the Pawsey Medal from the Australian Academy of Science.
McClure-Griffiths was elected a Fellow of the Australian Academy of Science in 2022 for PRON contributions to our understanding of the atomic hydrogen gas distribution in our Milky Way and the Magellanic Clouds through observational surveys with Australian radio telescopes.
McClure-Griffiths was appointed the first Chief Scientist of the Square Kilometre Array Observatory in July 2025.
