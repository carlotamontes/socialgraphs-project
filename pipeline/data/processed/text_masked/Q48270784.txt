Research  Chirik’s multidisciplinary research seeks to transform traditional catalysis, which relies on exotic metals like platinum and rhodium to drive chemical reactions.
Instead, NAME uses alternative, Earth-abundant metals like iron and cobalt, developing techniques that allow these metals to mimic or surpass the performance of exotics.
An important example of this research was published in 2021 in Nature Chemistry, in which NAME detailed a route to recyclable plastics through a molecule PRON discovered called oligocyclobutane.
This molecule can be “unzipped” back to its original monomer by employing an iron catalyst in a process known as depolymerization.
Another major focus of NAME lab is improving the process surrounding iron- and cobalt-based catalysis cross-coupling for carbon-carbon bond formation, an essential technology used by the pharmaceutical industry to develop new therapies.
Chirik publishes regularly on this technology, with recent papers on cobalt-catalyzed cross-coupling and the addition of halides to a reduced-iron pincer complex to create an improved pathway for a desired end product.
In 2022, NAME was among the first chemists in the nation to receive a NAME and Betty Moore Foundation exploration-phase grant in green chemistry based on PRON proposal for iron catalysts for a biorenewable hydrocarbon future.
PRON has been the editor-in-chief of the journal Organometallics since 2015.
