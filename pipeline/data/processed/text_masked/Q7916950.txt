NAME (; born February 13, 1965) is a physicist specializing in space plasma physics and a professor in the Department of Earth, Planetary, and Space Sciences at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA).
PRON research concerns particle acceleration, magnetic energy conversion in Earth's magnetosphere, and the interaction of the NAME with the solar wind.
PRON is the principal investigator of NASA's THEMIS and THEMIS–ARTEMIS missions and led the development and operation of UCLA's ELFIN CubeSat mission.
Career and research  Angelopoulos joined UCLA in 2007.
PRON research examines how energy supplied by the solar wind is stored and released in Earth's magnetosphere, how particles are accelerated, and how magnetospheric currents affect the upper atmosphere and ionosphere.
THEMIS and magnetic reconnection  As principal investigator of THEMIS (Time History of Events and Macroscale Interactions during Substorms), NAME led the development of a mission combining five spacecraft with over twenty ground-based observatories including magnetometers and all-sky imaging cameras.
The mission launched in February 2007 and is still active in an extended-science phase.
In a 2008 study led by Angelopoulos, simultaneous observations at multiple locations in Earth's magnetotail showed magnetic reconnection preceding auroral intensification and disruption of the near-Earth electric current.
The results supported tail reconnection as the trigger of magnetospheric substorms, addressing the competing explanations that substorms begin with reconnection farther down the magnetotail or current disruption nearer Earth.
PRON subsequent research has included identifying where magnetic energy is converted in the magnetotail.
THEMIS–ARTEMIS and the lunar plasma environment  NAME is also principal investigator of ARTEMIS (Acceleration, Reconnection, Turbulence and Electrodynamics of the Moon's Interaction with the Sun), which repurposed two THEMIS spacecraft to investigate the lunar plasma environment.
Transfer maneuvers began in 2009, followed by operations near the Earth–Moon Lagrange points in 2010.
The two spacecraft entered lunar orbit in June and July 2011.
The mission extended the scientific use of existing spacecraft from Earth's magnetosphere to the NAME.
Its two probes provide measurements of the solar wind, the NAME's plasma wake and the interaction of the NAME with Earth's magnetotail.
Simultaneous observations from two locations allow researchers to investigate plasma processes and particle acceleration around the NAME.
NAME's ARTEMIS research includes magnetic reconnection and turbulence in the magnetotail and solar wind, lunar crustal magnetic fields, and particle reflection near the NAME.
ELFIN and student training  At UCLA, NAME proposed and oversaw the development, launch and operation of the two ELFIN CubeSats, which deorbited in September 2022.
More than 400 UCLA undergraduates received training through the project under the mentorship of Angelopoulos and PRON research group.
The mission produced numerous peer-reviewed publications and ranks among the highest-impact CubeSat missions per dollar.
