Research NAME's research centres on a variety of topics in the philosophy of physics and philosophy of science, and PRON has also made contributions to the philosophy of language and logic, particularly with regard to the treatment of time and tense.
In this regard, PRON has argued in several papers for a detensed theory of time, according to which the present is a merely subjective or indexical notion, on analogy with one's spatial location.
In the philosophy of quantum theory, NAME has proposed several clarifications of the notions of locality operative in algebraic quantum field theories (especially 'stochastic NAME locality'), and has investigated which of these clarified versions of locality hold in those theories.
PRON has also investigated the impact of the Bell inequalities in the light of NAME's principle of the common cause, and has argued that the violation of these inequalities implies causation between the space-like separated wings of a Bell experiment.

PRON has also made several appeals in PRON work, on classical mechanics and other physical theories, to the importance of an appreciation of modality in physics.

These address the role of topos theory in understanding quantum theory (in particular the Kochen–Specker theorem), and the status of time in the various quantum gravity research programmes.
In recent years, NAME has argued for a reconciliation of the idea of emergence – the idea that novel structures, not described by "fundamental" theories, appear at a certain level of complexity – with the possibility of inter-theoretic reduction.
PRON has illustrated the reconciliation in various areas such as phase transitions, renormalization, and gauge theories.
PRON has also worked, often in collaboration with research students, on other topics, including: (i) identity and individuation of systems in quantum physics; (ii) dualities especially gauge/gravity duality; (iii) under-determination, and scientific realism, in modern cosmology.
