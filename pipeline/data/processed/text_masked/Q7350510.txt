Academic achievements NAME is among a group of pioneering physicists who helped create magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI), which today is the most widely used method of brain mapping.
In the 1980s, PRON worked with distinguished scientists including 2003 Nobel Prize winner GENDERWORD NAME to produce a mathematical framework for MRI coil design which was crucial to the development of ultra-fast echoplanar imaging (EPI).
This technique allows the recording of changes in blood flow in the brain associated with brain function and was crucial to the development of fMRI.
From 1988 until 1993 PRON worked as a researcher at the National Institutes of Health in Bethesda, MD.
Working with NAME le Bihan, a French neuroradiologist, PRON initially showed that EPI could be used to provide high quality maps of water diffusion in brain tissue, a discovery (known as Diffusion MRI) which has led to the widespread clinical use of MRI in stroke, where water diffusion in the affected brain tissue drops very rapidly after the ischemic event.
The technique also lies at the heart of diffusion tensor imaging, a method for non-invasive study of connecting pathways within the brain's white matter.
In 1991, still at NIH, PRON was the first to show that EPI could be used to monitor the time course of oxygenation changes in animal brain resulting from changes in the breathing gas.
This led to the discovery, made in collaboration with noted researcher NAME that EPI could accurately track within seconds the local changes in blood oxygenation in human brain (BOLD) caused by task-related neural activity.
For the first time, human brain activity could thus be observed entirely non-invasively, using the natural contrast agent of deoxyhaemoglobin.

In 1993 PRON returned to the United Kingdom as a Wellcome Principal Research Fellow to become head of MRI at the Wellcome Trust Centre for Neuroimaging at University College London,[17] a position PRON held from 1993 to 2003.
In 1994 PRON was awarded a professorship by University College London.[18] From 2006 until PRON retirement in 2014, PRON was director of the Department of Neurophysics at the Max Planck Institute for Human Cognitive and Brain Sciences, Leipzig.
PRON work there centred on the quest to gain more precise knowledge of the structure and function of the human brain, by using more powerful MRI scanners and improved hardware and methodology.[19] PRON continues to explore the implications for neuroscience and brain modelling of this improved knowledge, and PRON also contributes to the development of Neuroanthropology, which brings together insights from the study of culture and the study of the brain.[20]  Author of over 280 refereed articles in the fields of neurophysics, physics, anthropology and music, Turner has a Web of Science h-index of over 70, meaning PRON has authored a large number of highly cited academic papers.
