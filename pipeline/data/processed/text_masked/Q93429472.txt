Research   Discrete dipole approximation and DDSCAT  Flatau and Draine began developing DDSCAT in 1990 at Princeton University.
The software implements the discrete dipole approximation to calculate electromagnetic scattering and absorption by targets such as ice crystals, dust, soot, and marine phytoplankton.
Their 1994 description of the method appeared in the Journal of the Optical Society of America A. Later versions extended the method to periodic targets.
An independent review described DDSCAT as the publicly available program that helped popularize the discrete dipole approximation.
