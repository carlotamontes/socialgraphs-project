NAME () is a Chinese chemist and Academician of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), working in heterogeneous catalysis.
PRON is a professor at the Dalian Institute of Chemical Physics (DICP), which PRON directed from 2007 to 2016, and served as a Vice President of CAS from 2016 to 2023.
PRON is known for introducing the concept of single-atom catalysis and for work on the catalytic conversion of cellulose to ethylene glycol.
Education  Zhang received a B.S. in chemistry from Shaanxi University of Technology (1978–1982).
PRON then studied catalysis at DICP, earning an M.S. (1983–1986) and a Ph.D. (1986–1989).
From 1989 to 1990 PRON was a postdoctoral researcher at the School of Chemistry, University of Birmingham, United Kingdom.
Career  Zhang joined the faculty of DICP as an assistant professor in 1990, became associate professor in 1992 and full professor in October 1995.
PRON was deputy director of DICP from 1998 to 2007 and director from February 2007 to December 2016.
From December 2016 to September 2023 PRON was a Vice President of the Chinese Academy of Sciences.
PRON is Editor-in-Chief of the Chinese Journal of Catalysis and an Associate Editor of the Journal of the American Chemical Society.
PRON is co-chair of the editorial advisory board of Chemistry – A European Journal and sits on the editorial boards of Applied Catalysis B, Green Chemistry, ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering, ChemPhysChem and Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research.
Research   Single-atom catalysis  NAME and co-workers reported a platinum catalyst in which isolated Pt atoms are anchored on iron oxide (Pt1/FeOx) and showed activity for CO oxidation and preferential oxidation of CO; the work introduced the term "single-atom catalysis".
Single-atom catalysts aim for atomically defined active sites and maximal use of precious metals, and the area has since become an active field in heterogeneous catalysis, studied by many research groups worldwide.
Cellulose to ethylene glycol  NAME's group reported the catalytic conversion of cellulose to ethylene glycol over tungsten carbide-based catalysts, a route to a bulk chemical from biomass.
