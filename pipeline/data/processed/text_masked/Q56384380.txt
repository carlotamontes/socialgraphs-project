Research  In PRON early career, Wieland studied the relative arrangement of the two subunits of yeast fatty acid synthase, which is a large multienzyme complex, by combining antibody crosslinking with negative stain electron microscopy.
Using this approach, PRON developed a three-dimensional model of the enzyme in 1978.
At Regensburg, Wieland worked with NAME and NAME on the cell surface glycoprotein of the archaeon Halobacterium salinarum.
Through the 1980s PRON and PRON colleagues reported the structures of its glycans and aspects of their biosynthesis, sequenced a gene encoding a glycosylated archaeal surface layer protein, and found that glycosylation determines the shape of the cells.

Afterwards, PRON work focused on the COPI coat, which packages proteins into vesicles for transport between the Golgi apparatus and the endoplasmic reticulum.

Wieland and Brügger also examined how proteins and lipids interact in membranes, and, in 2012, reported that a single molecule of one specific species of sphingomyelin binds the transmembrane domain of the p24 coat subunit and regulates formation of the COPI coat.
