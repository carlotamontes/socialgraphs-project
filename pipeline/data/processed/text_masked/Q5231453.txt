NAME (born 26 November 1946) is an Australian physicist and professor at the University of Western Australia and Director of the Australian International Gravitational Research Centre.
NAME works on methods for the detection of gravitational waves.
PRON developed the niobium bar gravitational wave detector NIOBE, which achieved the lowest observed noise temperature, and participated in a worldwide collaboration that set the best limit on the burst events in 2001.
PRON has been responsible for numerous innovations including the 1984 invention of the first sapphire clock, a super-precise timepiece designed for space, as well as underpinning the research of the Frequency Stability Group at The University of Western Australia.
In 2003, together with NAME, NAME founded the Gravity Discovery Centre, a major centre for the promotion of science in Western Australia.
In 2010, NAME and collaborating partners developed an educational research program called the Science Education Enrichment Project, to research the benefits of specialist exhibition centres such as the Gravity Discovery Centre.
In 2014, NAME led the Einstein-First Project which aims to introduce Einsteinian Physics at an early age.
The project partners included Curtin University, Edith Cowan University, NAME (Polly) Farmer Foundation, U.S. Air Force Academy and the Gravity Discovery Centre.
Early life Born in Lymington, Hampshire, England in 1946, NAME emigrated to Australia with PRON family in 1950.
PRON received a Bachelor of Science degree from the University of Western Australia in 1968 and a PhD from the University of East Anglia in 1973, where PRON investigated superfluidity in liquid helium.
Honours and recognition NAME was awarded the Prime Minister's Prize for Science in 2020, and received a share - as a contributor - of the Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics in 2016.
PRON received the RSWA Medal from the Royal Society of Western Australia in 2018, the ANZAAS Medal from the Australian and New Zealand Association for the Advancement of Science in 2005, the Eureka Prize for Promoting Understanding of Science in 2005, and the Walter Boas Medal from the Australian Institute of Physics in 1995.
PRON was elected a Fellow of the American Physical Society in 2013, and a Fellow of the Australian Academy of Science in 2018.
NAME was appointed an Officer of the Order of Australia in the 2026 King's Birthday Honours in recognition of "distinguished service to physics, to precision measurement science, to gravitational wave research, and to scientific education".
