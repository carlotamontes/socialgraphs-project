Meers Oppenheim (born 1962, Bethesda, Maryland) is an American physicist who is Professor of Astronomy at Boston University.
PRON primary research interests include computational and theoretical space plasma physics, dynamics of the ionosphere and solar atmosphere, particle-wave interactions in plasmas, and the physics of meteor trails.
Education Oppenheim received PRON B.S. and M.Eng.
in 1984 and 1990 from Cornell University's School of Applied and Engineering Physics.
PRON then received PRON Ph.D. of Electrical Engineering in 1995 from Cornell's Space Plasma Physics Group.
Career Oppenheim began PRON career as a programmer and junior analyst at Jack Faucett Associates, Inc. in Chevy Chase, Maryland from 1980 to 1982.
From 1984 to 1986, PRON worked as a staff physicist at the Physics International Corporation in San Leandro, California.
In 1988, Oppenheim was a research assistant at Cornell University.
In 1995, PRON became a Max Planck Society Postdoctoral Scientist.
In 1996, PRON became a research associate at the University of Colorado, Boulder.
In 1998, Oppenheim began working as an assistant professor of astronomy at Boston University.
PRON became an associate professor in 2004, and in 2013, a full professor.
In 2018, PRON became the director of graduate admissions at Boston University.
Scientific contributions Oppenheim studies space plasma physics using supercomputer simulations, theory, and data.
PRON works on a range of research topics, including ionospheric and solar collisional plasmas, particle-wave interactions, and the physics of meteor trails.
Since 2016, PRON has been working to incorporate the important effects of ionospheric turbulence into planetary scale simulations of the coupled magnetosphere, ionosphere and atmosphere.
PRON has also been trying to model wave heating of the solar chromosphere.
Most recently, PRON has also been working on understanding the effects of UV photoelectrons on the ionosphere and their observational consequences.
