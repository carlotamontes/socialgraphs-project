NAME (; born 20 May 1945) is an Austrian quantum physicist and Nobel laureate in physics of 2022.
NAME is professor of physics emeritus at the University of Vienna and senior scientist at the Institute for Quantum Optics and Quantum Information of the Austrian Academy of Sciences.
Most of PRON research concerns the fundamental aspects and applications of quantum entanglement.

Research  Quantum teleportation NAME published one of the first realizations of quantum teleportation of an independent qubit.
PRON later expanded this work to developing a source for freely propagating teleported qubits and quantum teleportation over 144 kilometers between two Canary Islands.
Quantum teleportation is an essential concept in many quantum information protocols.
Besides its role for the transfer of quantum information, it is also considered as an important possible mechanism for building gates within quantum computers.
Entanglement swapping – teleportation of entanglement Entanglement swapping is the teleportation of an entangled state.
After its proposal, entanglement swapping was first realized experimentally by NAME's group in 1998.
It was then applied to carry out a delayed-choice entanglement swapping test.
Entanglement beyond two qubits – GHZ-states and their realizations  NAME contributed to the opening up of the field of multi-particle entanglement.
In 1990, PRON was the first with NAME and NAME to work on entanglement of more than two qubits.
The resulting GHZ theorem (see Greenberger–Horne–NAME state) is fundamental for quantum physics, as it provides the most succinct contradiction between local realism and the predictions of quantum mechanics.
GHZ states were the first instances of multi-particle entanglement ever investigated.
Finally, in 1999, PRON succeeded in providing the first experimental evidence of entanglement beyond two particles and also the first test of quantum nonlocality for GHZ states.
Quantum communication, quantum cryptography, quantum computation  In 1998 (published in 2000), PRON group was the first to implement quantum cryptography with entangled photons.
PRON then applied quantum entanglement to optical quantum computation, where in 2005, PRON performed the first implementation of one-way quantum computation.
This is a protocol based on quantum measurement as proposed by Knill, NAME and NAME.
The experiments of NAME and PRON group on the distribution of entanglement over large distances began with both free-space and fiber-based quantum communication and teleportation between laboratories located on the different sides of the river NAME.
This was then extended to larger distances across the city of Vienna and over 144 km between two Canary Islands, resulting in a successful demonstration that quantum communication with satellites is feasible.

Macroscopic quantum superposition NAME is also interested to extend quantum mechanics into the macroscopic domain.
In the early 1990s, PRON started experiments in the field of atom optics.
PRON developed a number of ways to coherently manipulate atomic beams, many of which, like the coherent energy shift of an atomic De Broglie wave upon diffraction at a time-modulated light wave, have become part of today's ultracold atom experiments.
In 1999, NAME abandoned atom optics for experiments with very complex and massive macro-molecules – fullerenes.
The successful demonstration of quantum interference for these C60 and C70 molecules in 1999 opened up a very active field of research.
In 2005, NAME with PRON group investigated the quantum physics of mechanical cantilevers.
In the year 2006 along with Heidmann in Paris and Kippenberg in Garching they demonstrated experimentally the self-cooling of a micro-mirror by radiation pressure, that is, without feedback.
Using orbital angular momentum states, PRON was able to demonstrate entanglement of angular momentum up to 300 ħ.
Further fundamental tests NAME's program of fundamental tests of quantum mechanics is aimed at implementing experimental realizations of many non-classical features of quantum physics for individual systems.
In 1998, PRON provided the final test of Bell's inequality closing the communication loophole by using superfast random number generators.
PRON group also realized the first Bell inequality experiment implementing the freedom-of-choice condition and provided the first realization of a Bell test without the fair sampling assumption for photons.
Among the further fundamental tests PRON performed the most notable one is PRON test of a large class of nonlocal realistic theories proposed by NAME.
The group of theories excluded by that experiment can be classified as those which allow reasonable subdivision of ensembles into sub-ensembles.
It goes significantly beyond Bell's theorem.
While Bell showed that a theory which is both local and realistic is at variance with quantum mechanics, NAME considered nonlocal realistic theories where the individual photons are assumed to carry polarization.
The resulting NAME inequality was shown to be violated in the experiments of the NAME group.
In an analogous way, PRON group showed that even quantum systems where entanglement is not possible exhibit non-classical features which cannot be explained by underlying non-contextual probability distributions.
