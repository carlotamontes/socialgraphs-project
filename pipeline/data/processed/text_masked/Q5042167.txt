NAME, born in Rio de Janeiro on July 19, 1956, is one of Brazil's most noted physicists and a member of the Brazilian Academy of Sciences.
The scientific director of the São Paulo Research Foundation (FAPESP)  and a full professor of quantum electronics at the Gleb Wataghin Physics Institute at the State University of Campinas (UNICAMP),  NAME is engaged in research in which PRON uses femtosecond lasers to study ultrafast phenomena.
Education NAME began PRON studies in Electrical Engineering at the Instituto Tecnológico da Aeronáutica (ITA) in 1974 and received PRON degree in 1978.
PRON received an M.Sc.
degree in physics in 1980 and a D.Sc.
degree in physics in 1983, both from the Gleb Wataghin Physics Institute at the Universidade Estadual de Campinas (State University at Campinas), popularly known as UNICAMP.
NAME and research In 1981-82 PRON was a researcher at Istituto Italo Latino Americano, Laboratório de Óptica Quântic at the University of Rome.
PRON was named a “Professor MS4” in Physics at UNICAMP in 1986.
In 1986-87 PRON was a researcher at AT&T Bell Laboratories in Holmdel, New Jersey.
In 1989 PRON was named a “Professor NAME” in Physics at UNICAMP.
In 1989 PRON was visiting professor at the Laboratoire de Physique des Solides de l´Université de Paris V; in 1990 PRON was a researcher at AT&T Bell Laboratories in Murray Hill, New Jersey.
In 1994 PRON was promoted to “Professor MS6” at UNICAMP, and continues to hold that title.
PRON was Director of the Physics Institute at UNICAMP from 1991 to 1994 and from 1998 to the present.
PRON was Dean of Research at UNICAMP from 1994 to 1998, and from April 2002 to April 2005 was Rector of UNICAMP.
PRON is a member of the Superior Council of FAPESP, has been its president twice (1996–98 and 1998–2000), and since April 2005 PRON has been its Scientific Director.
FAPESP is a foundation that is supported by NAME taxpayers and that is one of Brazil's leading funders of scientific research.
At FAPESP PRON oversees the review of 18,000 research proposals every year.
Under PRON direction FAPESP has supported research in all scientific disciplines and organized special programs in Bioenergy and Global Climate Change.
NAME and PRON team have shown “that 6 femtosecond pulses, which are only three optical cycles long, could be generated by compensating dispersion to third-order.
These short pulses allowed studies to be done in bacteriorhodopsin, organic dyes and semiconductor films.”
Other professional activities From 1995 to 1999 PRON was vice-president of the Brazilian Physical Society (SBF); PRON  has served as editor of the Revista Brasileira de Física Aplicada e Instrumentação.
PRON was also coordinator or director of several events sponsored by the International Center for Theoretical Physics (ICTP) in Trieste and was a member of the International Advisory Committee of the Optical Society of America.
In 2010 NAME was a member of the 12-member special committee formed by the Inter Academy Council, at the request of the U.N. Secretary General, to review the procedures of IPCC.
NAME gave the keynote address at a 2012 event at the Woodrow Wilson International Center for Scholars in Washington, D.C. PRON argued that Brazil needs not only higher funding levels for university-level research, but also “more institutions devoted to higher learning and research, with more researchers.
Per million inhabitants, Brazil has one-fourth the number of researchers compared to Spain, and one-eighth the number in South Korea.”
PRON suggested that the establishment of new universities financed by federal-state collaboration could result in “institutions that rank among the hundred best in the world in ten years” and could create “opportunities for more young people while developing science and technology in Brazil.”
Also in 2012, PRON gave a plenary session at the Latin America Optics & Photonics Conference (LAOP) in São Sebastião, Brazil.
PRON subject was “Science and Technology in Brazil.”
In the same year PRON visited Madrid in an effort to promote collaboration between scientists and science educators in Brazil and Spain.
