PRON has been  Head of Inorganic Chemistry and Co-Director of the Centre for Radiochemistry Research at the University of Manchester since 2015, and Director of the National Nuclear User Facility at the Centre for Radiochemistry Research since 2019.

Career and research   After postdoctoral fellowships with NAME at the University of Edinburgh, NAME at Newcastle University as the Wilfred Hall Research Fellow, and NAME at the University of Nottingham, NAME began PRON independent academic career at the University of Nottingham with a Royal Society University Research Fellowship (2007–2015) held with a proleptic NAME.
PRON was promoted to Associate Professor and NAME in 2010 and Professor of Inorganic Chemistry in 2013.
PRON moved to the University of Manchester in 2015 as Head of Inorganic Chemistry and Co-Director of the Centre for Radiochemistry Research.
PRON is Director of the National Nuclear User Facility at the Centre for Radiochemistry Research since 2019, and PRON held an Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council Established Career Fellowship (2015–2021).
PRON was GENDERWORD of COST Action CM1006, a 22-country research network of over 120 research groups in f-block chemistry (2011–2015), is an advisor to the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission (2013–), and was an elected category 3 member of Senate, the University of Manchester (2016–2019).
NAME's research is focused on synthetic inorganic chemistry, particularly making early transition metal, lanthanide, and actinide complexes to explore their structure, bonding, reactivity, and magnetism.
In 2011 PRON reported a single-molecule magnet based on depleted uranium.
In 2012 PRON research group was the first to synthesize a molecular terminal uranium(V) nitride.
In 2013 PRON reported the isolation of a terminal uranium(VI) nitride akin to examples previously restricted to cryogenic matrix isolation experiments, and that formed the basis from which to report the electronic structure of uranium nitrides including quantifying the covalence of the uranium-nitrogen triple bond by using 15N NMR spectroscopy.
In 2013, PRON research group disclosed the first f-element cyclobutadienyl complexes.
Between 2014-2025 PRON reported a range of M=EH bonds (M = Th, U; E = N, P, As, and Sb) along with phosphide, arsenido, and stibido derivatives.
In 2019 PRON research group isolated a uranium(V)-dinitrogen complex.
In 2021, Liddle reported a tri-thorium cluster featuring two-electron σ-aromatic actinide metal-metal bonding, and in follow-up work reported one-electron analogues that exhibit exalted diamagnetism and were found to be open-shell aromatic superatoms.
Before the synthesis of those metal-metal complexes, examples of actinide-actinide bonding had been restricted to matrix isolation experiments and fullerene-encapsulated species.
In 2022, PRON reported a terminal neptunium(V)-mono(oxo) complex.
Before the synthesis of this complex all transuranium-ligand multiple bond complexes required two or more metal-ligand multiple bonds to be stable.
Following on from PRON reports of U=C double bonds over uranium oxidation states +3 to +6, in 2022 and 2024 PRON reported neptunium- and plutonium- diphosphonioalkylidenes and -N -heterocyclic carbenes.
In 2018 PRON introduced titanium as a catalytically active metal in molecular dinitrogen to ammonia catalysis chemistry, and in 2025, in collaboration with the Mazzanti group at École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne, PRON reported catalytic conversion of side-on bound dinitrogen to ammonia at uranium.
PRON has also reported investigations on the basic properties of actinides, including homologation of carbon monoxide, showing that the inverse-trans-influence is general, reversible oxidative addition/reductive elimination at uranium, demonstrating the structure-directing role of f-orbital overlap-driven covalency, establishing pushing-from-below for thorium, introducing Frustrated Lewis Pair chemistry to the actinides, explaining anomalous magnetism of uranium(IV), inverting the fundamental disproportionation chemistry of uranium, and using actinides to stabilise unusual main group species.
