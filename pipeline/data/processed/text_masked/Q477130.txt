NAME  (born May 11, 1959) is an American physicist and director at the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics in Hannover, Germany, and founder and leader of the distributed volunteer computing project Einstein@Home project.
PRON is an honorary physics professor at Leibniz University Hannover, an adjunct physics professor at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee, and also the initiator / project leader of smartmontools hard disk utility.
PRON has done research work on models of the very early universe (inflationary cosmology, cosmic strings), the detection and data analysis of gravitational waves, and has expertise in the development and operation of large computer clusters.
NAME currently leads a research group working on the detection of gravitational waves in data from ground-based interferometric detectors and from pulsar timing arrays, and on radio, gamma-ray and gravitational-wave signals from rotating neutron stars.
NAME was one of the first scientists to become aware of the initial detection of GW150914 at LIGO, in September 2015.
NAME's research work has been supported by the US National Science Foundation between 1987 and 2018.
Early life and education  NAME graduated from Wayland High School, Wayland, Massachusetts, US, in 1976.
PRON obtained PRON Bachelor of Science in physics in 1980 at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology under the supervision of NAME.
NAME completed PRON PhD in physics in 1984 at University of Cambridge with PRON thesis "Vacuum Energy and General Relativity" under the supervision of NAME.
From 1983 to 1985 PRON was a postdoctoral fellow at the University of California, Santa Barbara and from 1985 to 1986 at Tufts University.
From 1986 to 1987 PRON was a chercheur associé (research associate) at Paris Observatory in Meudon.
Career and research  In 1987 NAME became a research assistant professor at Tufts University.
PRON joined the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee in 1989 as assistant professor of physics, was promoted to associate professor of physics in 1992 and to a full professor of physics in 1997.
In 2007 PRON became a director at the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics (NAME) in Hanover, Germany and an adjunct professor at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee.
Since 2008 PRON is also an honorary professor of physics at Leibniz University Hannover.
NAME has worked on early universe cosmology, inflationary models of the early universe, properties of de Sitter space, and curved-space quantum field theory.
PRON further worked on networks of cosmic strings and the gravitational radiation emitted by them.
PRON made contributions to the detection and data analysis of gravitational waves of different types: from a stochastic background, from inspiraling compact binaries, and continuous, near-sinusoidal signals.
NAME was a member of the executive committee of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration from 1997 to 2018.
PRON also worked on designing a runtime system for volunteer computing, and on using the Einstein@Home project to discover new radio and gamma-ray pulsars in data from large radio telescopes and the Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope.
PRON has also worked data analysis for pulsar timing arrays.
This includes research on the variance of the Hellings-Downs correlation, which is a central measure for the detection of gravitational waves with pulsar timing arrays.
Between 1987 and 2018, NAME research has been supported by the US National Science Foundation through 14 grants totaling approximately $10 million.
