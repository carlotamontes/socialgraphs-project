NAME (born July 1954) is a U.S.-Swiss physicist of Czech origin and researcher in nanoscience and nanotechnology.
PRON is Emeritus Professor of Physics at Michigan State University.
PRON is known for predicting the structure and calculating properties of surfaces, atomic clusters including the C60 buckminsterfullerene, nanotubes, nanowires and nanohelices, graphene, and two-dimensional materials including phosphorene.
Academic career  Tománek earned a doctoral degree in Physics from the Freie Universität Berlin in 1983 under the supervision of NAME and became Hochschulassistent there in 1984.
Between 1985 and 1987 PRON worked as postdoctoral researcher at the Bell Labs under the supervision of NAME and at the University of California, Berkeley under the supervision of NAME.
Since 1987, PRON has been Professor of Physics at Michigan State University, where PRON directs the Computational Nanotechnology Laboratory at the Department of Physics and Astronomy.
Research  Tománek and PRON research group have worked in areas in nanoscience and nanotechnology.
As a graduate student at FU Berlin, PRON studied structural end electronic properties of surfaces, including reconstruction and photoemission spectra.
PRON was intrigued by the unusual structure and electronic properties of atomic clusters, including collective electronic excitations and superconductivity.
PRON computational studies of growth regimes of silicon and carbon clusters have made use of the semi-quantitative Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals (LCAO) or tight-binding method.
During PRON 1994 sabbatical stay at the laboratory of NAME, PRON turned PRON interest to the unique properties of nanotubes formed of carbon (CNTs) and other materials.
PRON studied their morphology, formation, mechanical stiffness, their ability to conduct heat and electrons, and field electron emission.
After 2000, PRON got involved in studies of two-dimensional materials including phosphorene.
In the following years, PRON has continued identifying applications of carbon nanotubes and two-dimensional materials in fields including low-resistance contacts to nanostructures, nanomechanical energy storage, and purification and desalination of water.
Conferences  Tománek initiated a series of annual NAME (NT) conferences and a Gordon Research Conference on Two-dimensional electronics beyond graphene.
Honors and awards  In 2004 Tománek was elected a Fellow of the American Physical Society and in 2005 PRON received the prestigious NAME Scientist Award (Germany).
In 2008 PRON received the Japan Carbon Award for Life-Time Achievement and was chosen by the American Physical Society as member of the Outstanding Referees Program for excellence in peer review.
In 2016 PRON received the Lee Hsun Research Award for Materials Science from the Chinese Academy of Sciences.
