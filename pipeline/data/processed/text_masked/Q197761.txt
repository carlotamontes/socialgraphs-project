NAME (; born 1962) is a Chinese-American experimental condensed matter physicist.
PRON is the Paul Pigott Professor of Physical Sciences and a professor of applied physics and of physics at Stanford University, and a senior fellow of Stanford’s Precourt Institute for Energy.
PRON is particularly noted for PRON angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) studies on high-temperature superconductors.
PRON is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and a Fellow of the Royal Society.
Life Shen was born in 1962 in Zhejiang, China.
PRON graduated from Fudan University with a B.S. in 1983, and went to the United States through the CUSPEA program organized by NAME.
PRON earned PRON M.S. degree in 1985 at Rutgers University.
In 1989 PRON received a PhD in applied physics from Stanford University under NAME and NAME.
In 1991 PRON became assistant professor, in 1996 associate professor, and in 2000 full professor at Stanford University.
At SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory PRON was chief scientist from 2010 to 2013 and science and technology advisor from 2013 to 2019.
PRON was the founding director of the Stanford Institute for Materials and Energy Sciences (SIMES) from 2006 to 2011.
Furthermore, from 2005 to 2008 PRON was director of the Geballe Laboratory for Advanced Materials.
PRON has held the Paul Pigott Professorship in Physical Sciences since 2006 and is a senior fellow of Stanford’s Precourt Institute for Energy.
Research Shen’s group uses angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy and other photon-based techniques, including soft X-ray scattering and time- and spin-resolved spectroscopy, to study quantum materials such as superconductors, magnets and topological insulators.
In 1993, NAME and co-workers reported a strongly anisotropic superconducting gap in NAME (Bi2212), consistent with d-wave pairing.
Over the following decade, NAME and collaborators traced how the electronic structure of a strongly correlated NAME insulator evolves with carrier doping into anomalous metallic and superconducting states.
Their studies progressed from measurements of band dispersion in undoped cuprates and electronic reorganization upon doping, published in 1995, to the identification in 1996 of a momentum-dependent normal-state excitation gap in underdoped samples, leaving only a segment of the Fermi surface, now known as the pseudogap and Fermi arc.
Beginning in 2001, Shen’s group also reported evidence for strong electron-phonon coupling in cuprates.
In 2004, they identified coupling between electrons and the B1g oxygen-buckling phonon in the antinodal regions of Bi2212, where the superconducting gap is largest.
In 2018, they reported evidence suggesting that electron-phonon coupling and electronic correlations act cooperatively to enhance superconductivity.
Studies of doped one-dimensional cuprates further elucidated the interplay between electron-phonon coupling and NAME physics.
PRON group’s measurements of Sr2RuO4 and Sr2RhO4 demonstrated quantitative agreement with bulk electronic and thermodynamic properties.
In 2022, the group further established a connection between ARPES measurements and thermodynamic properties in cuprates.
NAME and collaborators extended these methods to a broader range of quantum materials, including iron-based superconductors, correlated oxides, topological insulators and low-dimensional systems.
In 2009, they experimentally identified Bi2Te3 as a three-dimensional topological insulator with a single surface Dirac cone.
In 2017, they reported evidence for a quantum spin Hall state in monolayer 1T′-WTe2.
Their work also explored the interplay between lattice vibrations and electronic correlations.
In 2014, the group reported evidence that coupling to phonons in the SrTiO3 substrate substantially enhances superconductivity in monolayer FeSe.
In 2017, combined time-resolved photoemission and X-ray diffraction measurements quantified electron-phonon coupling in FeSe and demonstrated its enhancement by electronic correlations.
NAME’s group also developed cantilever-based scanning microwave impedance microscopy, a near-field technique for imaging local electrical properties at the nanoscale.
PRON co-authored the 2010 proposal of photon-enhanced thermionic emission for concentrated solar-energy conversion.
NAME has trained numerous graduate students and postdoctoral researchers who have gone on to lead independent research groups at universities and research institutions worldwide.
