Early life and education  NAME was born in Frankfurt.
PRON studied mathematics and physics at Goethe University Frankfurt.
Hossenfelder later earned a doctorate from the same university in theoretical physics in 2003; PRON dissertation, supervised by NAME, studied microscopic black-hole production in models with large extra dimensions.
Academic career  After post-doctoral posts at the GSI Helmholtz Centre for Heavy Ion Research, University of Arizona, UC Santa Barbara and Perimeter Institute in Canada, PRON joined NORDITA in Stockholm as an assistant professor in 2009.
In 2015, PRON moved to the Frankfurt Institute for Advanced Studies, where PRON led the Analog Systems for Gravity Duals group and, in 2019, received the institute's inaugural Award for Innovative Thinking.
In 2023, PRON became affiliated with the LMU Munich Center for Mathematical Philosophy as a visiting researcher, studying the role of locality and fine-tuning in quantum-mechanical foundations.
In September 2025, NAME said that PRON was no longer affiliated with the center.
NAME has published research on topics such as quantum gravity, black holes, and dark matter.
Public engagement  NAME has written the popular-science blog Backreaction since 2006 and has contributed articles to Scientific American, New Scientist and Quanta Magazine.
PRON first trade book, Lost in Math: How Beauty Leads Physics Astray (Basic Books, 2018), argues that an aesthetic preference for mathematically "beautiful" theories has hindered progress in fundamental physics.
PRON follow-up, Existential Physics: A Scientist's Guide to Life's Biggest Questions, was published by Viking in 2022, exploring what physics can say about questions such as free will, consciousness and the origin of the universe.
In a positive review, NAME stated that the book suggests that "many spiritual ideas are compatible with modern physics".
During the COVID-19 pandemic in the early 2020s, NAME began focusing on YouTube content explaining science in simple terms.
By September 2023, PRON channel had reached one million subscribers, and NPR reported that income from YouTube helped fund PRON physics research.
By November 2024, creating YouTube videos had become PRON main activity, while PRON devoted less time to physics research.
As of September 2026, PRON channel had reached 1.79 million subscribers and approximately 378 million total views.
NAME is known for PRON strong criticism of modern academic physics research.
NPR described NAME's views on modern physics research as "somewhat contrarian".
PRON has written op-eds in The Guardian and The New York Times critiquing particle physics and the search for hypothesized particles using colliders.
Responding in a published letter, cosmologist NAME argued that unsuccessful searches also advance science by testing hypotheses and excluding possibilities.
Hossenfelder has been criticised by science communicators for PRON anti-establishment and pessimistic presentation of academia, arguing that it could aid science denialists.
In 2024, NAME described string theory as "undead", but weeks later said that a paper using bootstrapping methods strengthened the argument for the theory.
Writing for McGill University's Office for Science and Society in April 2025, NAME praised NAME's ability to explain complex science but criticised PRON broader claims about academic research.
PRON argued that PRON criticism had expanded from particle physics to claims that science itself was failing, and that PRON portrayal of publicly funded research as a scam was misleading and risked undermining public trust in science.
PRON also wrote that PRON videos criticising academic research tended to attract more views than PRON educational videos.
In a September 2025 essay in The Wall Street Journal, NAME described NAME as part of a genre of "conspiracy physics" YouTubers who portray academic physics as a corrupt establishment.
