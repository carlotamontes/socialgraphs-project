NAME (born 5 July 1956 in Zürich) is a Swiss experimental physicist and professor at the University of Basel working on nanoscience and nanoelectronics.
Biography and career Schönenberger studied electrical engineering and obtained PRON degree in 1979.
While working as an engineer in a research lab at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Zurich, PRON became interested in natural science and studied physics, obtaining PRON diploma from the institute in 1986.
As a graduate student, PRON worked under the supervision of NAME (Nobel Prize laureate in 1986) and NAME at the IBM Research Laboratory at Rüschlikon and received PRON PhD in physics with a thesis on magnetic force microscopy in 1990.
PRON then joined the Philips Research Laboratories at Eindhoven in the Netherlands as a postdoctoral fellow and later as a permanent staff member.
In 1995 PRON was appointed full professor (of experimental physics) at the University of Basel, where PRON heads the Nanoelectronics Group.
PRON led the Swiss Nanoscience Institute (SNI) between 2006 and 2022 and become honorary member of the SNI in 2022.
Research After PRON early work on magnetic force microscopy, Schönenberger then used different force microscopy techniques to study single charges and, in particular, single electron tunneling.
PRON studied electron transport in quantum wires and shot noise and noise reduction in electron transport.
Subsequently, PRON and PRON group studied transport in increasingly smaller natural and engineered nanoscale devices operating in the quantum regime.
These include quasi one-dimensional objects such as quantum wires, carbon nanotubes, and DNA-molecules or two-dimensional graphene.
In 1999 PRON published three of PRON most-cited and most impactful papers that report key experiments in nanoelectronics.
PRON demonstrated electronic quantum interference by measuring the NAME–NAME effect in multi-walled carbon nanotubes and by realizing the quantum optical Hanbury Brown and Twiss effect for the first time with electrons and demonstrated the anti-bunching effects arising from their fermionic statistics and PRON demonstrated electron transport through DNA molecules.
In particular, PRON and PRON group are a leader in the study of electronic properties of "hybrid" devices, that combine normal metals with superconductors and ferromagnetic elements.
The latter introduce by the proximity effect non-trivial correlations such as a pairing or exchange field.
This can give rise to new correlated many-body quantum states.
Examples are topological states, molecular Andreev-bound states and Majorana-like states.
Other applications include the generation of spatially separated entangled electrons using a Cooper pair splitter.
More recently, the group investigates ultra-clean and suspended devices, which allow to couple the electrical and mechanical degrees of freedom of the device at the quantum limit.
