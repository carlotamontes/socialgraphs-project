Research  NAME is known primarily for PRON research on porphyrins and porphyrin analogues.
In the 1990s, NAME reported some of the earliest large-scale ab initio and density functional theory calculations on porphyrins and other bioinorganic systems.
In particular, PRON worked on the problem of diatomic ligand discrimination by heme proteins, arguing against the then-prevalent steric model and emphasizing the key role of hydrogen bonding in the distal pocket.
Transition metal spin state energetics.
In the 1990s, NAME showed that common exchange-correlation functionals yield highly divergent results on the energetics of low-energy spin states of transition metal complexes.
PRON showed that classic pure functionals tend to unduly favor lower-spin states, while hybrid functionals such as B3LYP err in the other ditrection, favoring higher-spin states.
Subsequently, PRON showed that GGA functionals based on the OPTX exchange functional often provide a good solution to the problem, as does B3LYP* (with 15% exact exchange).
NAME has made numerous studies of the phenomenon of ligand noninnocence, particularly as it pertains to transition metal corrole derivatives.
PRON has also used multiconfigurational ab initio calculations to tackle the longstanding question of local oxidation states in transition metal nitrosyls.
Since 2010, NAME has developed the field of heavy element coordination chemistry of corroles, focusing on 4d or 5d transition metal derivatives.
PRON reported some of the first examples of 99Tc, rhenium, osmium, platinum, and gold corroles.
A number of these complexes exhibit near-IR phosphorescence and have found applications as photosensitizers in oxygen sensing, photodynamic therapy, anddye-sensitized solar cells.
Low-barrier hydrogen bonds and tautomerism.
Early in PRON career, NAME used X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) to study short-strong hydrogen bonds in porphyrin-type molecules.
Subsequently, PRON reported the first example of a stable cis tautomer of a free-base porphyrin in the form of a termolecular hydrogen-bonded complex, ultimately formulating a general approach to synthesizing such species.
NAME has contributed significantly to a number of other problems in physical organic and physical inorganic chemistry, including transition metal complexes with unusually low and high coordination numbers, stereochemistry of sandwich compounds, metal-metal quadruple bonds and quintuple bonds, transition metal carbides, low-valent main-group species, electronic consequences of scandide contraction, and the perfluoro cage effect.
