Work   Since moving into applied optics, Ritsch-Marte has contributed to the development and application of microscopic methods and optical tweezers.
PRON research group has pioneered new usages for spatial light modulators (SLM) in the form of liquid-crystal displays to optical microscopy.
As a programmable NAME filters, SLMs allow rapid switching between different microscopy modalities (bright field, dark field, phase contrast) without the need for changing any hardware components.
In the scope of the ERC Advanced Investigator NAME (= Coherently Advanced Tissue and Cell Holographic Imaging and Trapping) PRON group also developed methods of optical manipulation of ever larger particles, e.g. the optical "macro-tweezers" system, a large volume dual-beam mirror trap.
This dual-beam approach is suitable to trap and guide swimming microorganisms such as NAME without inducing any optical damage.
For even larger particles, a combination of optical tweezers and an ultrasonic trap was developed.
An additional field of research in Ritsch-Marte's group is non-linear microscopy, e.g. chemically-selective coherent anti-Stokes Raman scattering (CARS), in particular they have developed a non-scanning (wide-field) CARS variant.
Ritsch-Marte and PRON colleague NAME hold several patents (e.g. spiral-phase contrast microscopy or a diffractive Moiré lens with tunable refraction index).
