NAME  (born 25 August 1976) is a Scottish physicist who is Professor of Physics and director of the Centre for Biophotonics at the University of Strathclyde.
PRON is interested in optical microscopy and novel imaging techniques, and leads the Mesolens microscope facility where PRON research investigates linear and non-linear optics.
Early life and education  NAME credits PRON high school physics teacher with PRON inspiration to study science.
PRON studied optoelectronics and laser physics at the University of Strathclyde, where PRON was taught by NAME.
PRON remained there for PRON graduate studies, earning a PhD in laser technology under the supervision of NAME in 2002.
PRON was the first member of PRON family to go to university.
Career and research NAME almost worked in telecommunications, but was convinced by Ferguson to join NAME's new Centre for Biophotonics.
PRON became interested in biomedical research and increasingly aware of the limitations of commercial imaging.
Here PRON worked with Alison Gurney on the development of confocal, multi-photon wide-field microscopes.
Gurney encouraged NAME to apply for fellowships, and PRON was a Royal Society of Edinburgh and Research Councils UK (RCUK) postdoctoral fellow.
PRON developed the world's first white light supercontinuum laser that could be used for confocal microscopy, as well as laser scanning fluorescence microscopy.
PRON attended the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) Practical Course in Advanced Optical Microscopy in Plymouth, which PRON has continued to support throughout PRON academic career.
NAME directs the Centre for Biophotonics and Mesolens laboratory at the University of Strathclyde, working on nonlinear and linear optical instrumentation for biomedical imaging.
Nonlinear optics allows physicists precise control of excitation parameters, including the chance to tune the duration of laser pules.
In 2009, NAME began working with NAME and built a new lens, NAME, that can allow 3D imaging with a depth resolution of a few microns for objects up to 6 mm wide and 3 mm thick.
The NAME is a giant optical microscope objective supported by the Medical Research Council (MRC).
It can be used to image large biomedical specimens, including embryos, tumours and areas in brain, as well as scanning large areas of samples in a short amount of time.
The lens has 260 megapixel effective camera and a magic ratio of 8:1, which can even resolve individual bacteria.
As the photometric volume can sample such a large area with sub-cellular detail, the Mesolens may allow for the imaging of rare events.
Mesolens became a University spin-off, but NAME decided to stay in academia to explore the physics of biomedical processes.
The NAME generates such large amounts of data that NAME became interested in computational biology.
The NAME was selected by Physics World as one of the top achievements of 2016.
PRON discussed the Mesolens on the podcast Not Exactly Rocket Science.
Alongside the Mesolens, NAME has explored how laser sources can be used to open voltage-gated ion channels, such as Calcium-activated potassium channels.
PRON has developed a fast-acquisition version of two-photon excitation microscopy that can be used to image at rates of 100 frames/second.
PRON created polymer NAME beads that are responsive to enzymes.
PRON is working with the Medical Research Scotland to create high brightness light-emitting diodes.
In May 2012, PRON was appointed Professor and Director of the Centre for Biophotonics at the University of Strathclyde.
PRON leads the Strathclyde Theme of Physics and Life Sciences and is part of the Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council (EPSRC) Centre for Doctoral Training in Optical Medical Imaging.
Awards and honours In recognition of PRON work, NAME was elected a Fellow of the Institute of Physics (FInstP) in 2010, a Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh (FRSE) in 2019 and a Fellow of the Royal Microscopical Society (FRMS).
