NAME is known for inventing the drug pregabalin as a possible treatment for epileptic seizures.
During 1988-1990, NAME, a visiting research fellow, synthesized a series of molecules for NAME.
The molecule was transported into the brain, where it activated the enzyme L-glutamate decarboxylase.
NAME anticipated that the enzyme would increase production of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA and block convulsions.
Eventually, the set of molecules were sent to Parke-Davis Pharmaceuticals for testing.
In addition to treating seizures, pregabalin was found to be effective in treating fibromyalgia pain, neuropathic pain, and generalized anxiety disorder.
It was approved by the FDA in 2004 and marketed by NAME (which bought Parke-Davis) under the trade name Lyrica.
Research has shown that the mechanism of the drug is more complicated than originally anticipated.
In addition to its effects on GABA-AT substrate behavior, pregabalin binds to calcium channels and blocks glutamate release.
GABA is potentiated, but through a different mechanism than originally suspected.
