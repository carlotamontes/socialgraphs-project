NAME (July 24, 1936 – April 19, 2026) was a German-born American inorganic chemist.
PRON was known for developing safe methods of studying highly reactive and dangerous chemicals, while PRON extensive experience in fluorine chemistry has earned PRON the title of 'The Fluorine God'.
PRON research covered fluorine chemistry of nitrogen and NAME and the synthesis of new energetic materials.
Background In 1957 Christe began PRON chemistry studies at the University of Stuttgart.
PRON prepared both PRON diploma (1960) and PhD theses (1961) under the supervision of NAME.
In 1962, Christe immigrated to the US and settled in California.
PRON started working as a senior research chemist with Stauffer Chemical Co in Richmond, California.
In 1967 PRON joined Rocketdyne Division Rockwell International in Canoga Park where PRON became the manager of research in 1978.
In 1994 PRON accepted split positions between the Air Force Research Laboratory at Edwards Air Force Base and the Loker Hydrocarbon Research Institute of University of Southern California (USC).
In 2005, PRON moved full-time to USC where PRON is working as a research professor.
Christe died on April 19, 2026, at the age of 89.
Work From the 1960s, Christe investigated the synthesis of new fluorides of nitrogen, halogen, and the halogen oxides, as well as their ions.
Notable examples are the syntheses of both NF4+ and ClF6+.
On the occasion of the 100th anniversary of Henri Moissan's first production of fluorine, Christe in 1986 reported about the first truly chemical synthesis of fluorine.
Another important field of PRON work covered the research on homoleptic nitrogen compounds for use in energetic materials.
In this context, PRON synthesized the bent pentazenium cation, N5+, and was the first to experimentally detect the cyclic pentazolate anion, N5− and to prepare stable high nitrogen pentazolates.
Both compounds are considered important milestones on the way to even heavier polynitrogen compounds.
In 1996, Christe was the first to propose the use of ionic liquids as energetic materials in propulsion systems.
Before retiring from USC PRON was working on the development of oxidizers to replace the dangerous ammonium perchlorate.
Christe has authored over 400 peer-review publications, and held more than 60 patents.
In 2025, remarking on the discovery of the hexanitrogen molecule, the most energetic molecule yet made, Christe wrote: "This work is spectacular and, in my opinion, is worthy of a Nobel  prize."
PRON continued: "Compared to carbon chemistry, where two Nobel prizes were awarded for the discovery of two new carbon allotropes (buckyballs and graphene), the experimental observation of a neutral molecular nitrogen allotrope is orders of magnitude more difficult."
