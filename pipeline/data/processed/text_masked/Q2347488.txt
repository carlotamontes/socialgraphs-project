PPPL director  On 1 July 2018, PRON was appointed director of the Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory (PPPL).
As director, Cowley's policy has been to de-emphasize nuclear fusion and high temperature plasma research, turning PPPL into a broad-based laboratory for plasma physics, including low temperature plasmas.
This effort involved the construction of a new building at PPPL, the Princeton Plasma Innovation Center (PPIC).
PPIC provides laboratory space for plasma-related research areas new to PPPL, including microelectronics, quantum sensors and devices, and sustainability sciences, as well as housing fusion theoretical and computational scientists.
PRON stated PRON planned for low temperature plasma science to account for 30-50% of the total in future, compared to prior fusion predominance.
At the same time as de-emphasizing fusion, PRON main stated priority has been to recover the NSTX-U tokamak from its 2016 failure.
NSTX-U was the US's flagship fusion experiment, and its failure under Cowley's predecessor "strained the lab's relations with DOE" As of August 2020, NSTX-U was expected to re-start in late 2021.

Cowley has also expressed a strong commitment to basic science research, including astrophysics.
At the unveiling of the Facility for Laboratory Reconnection Experiments (FLARE) in June 2025, Cowley said the experiment – which is designed to study magnetic reconnection —  represents the next generation of research into fundamental plasma physics and will serve as a collaborative research platform for scientists worldwide.
Cowley supports integrating artificial intelligence (AI) into fusion system design.
PRON told an audience at PRON University of Oxford Lamb Lecture that integrated modeling systems "with multiscale simulations, plus AI surrogates, plus simulations of the neutrons, simulations of the damage, and simulations of the engineering to try to design a better fusion system" are needed to design the ideal fusion system.
StellFoundry is one such project at PPPL, using AI to try to find the ideal configuration for stellarators.
Cowley has also argued in favor of larger investment in physical fusion experiments, arguing that "a digital solution is not enough", calling for "investments in the tens of billions of dollars" to build a fusion pilot plant.
PRON also advocated developing a highly skilled workforce and international collaborations.
PRON stated the belief that "clean, safe, sustainable fusion reactors" will provide baseload power generation "by the end of this [21st] century".
