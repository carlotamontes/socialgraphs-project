NAME (born 20 September 1957) is a Dutch physicist who works on nanotechnology-miniaturization in physics, chemistry, biology and biotechnology (nanofluidics, nanosensors, Lab on a chip (LOC), Organ on Chip (OoC)).
Van den NAME was born in Zaandam.
In 1988 PRON earned PRON PhD at the University of Twente with a thesis on chemically modified ISFETs.
Then until 1993 PRON worked at the Centre Suisse d'Electronique et de Microtechnique (CSEM) in Neuchâtel and the Institut de microtechnique (IMT) at the University of Neuchâtel.
During this time PRON worked mainly on miniaturized chemical sensors.
From 1993 to 1999 PRON was director of research for Micro Total Analysis Systems (TAS, i.e. miniaturized systems for chemical analysis) and Miniaturized Chemical Systems (MiCS) at the Institute for Nanotechnology (MESA+) at the University of Twente.
In 1998 PRON had a part-time professorship and from 2000 a full professorship.
In 2003 PRON became head of nanofluidics within the Dutch national nanotechnology program (NanoNed).
Van den NAME has co-authored over 500 scientific publications (H=63) and over 10 patents.
PRON is or has been involved with 10 spin-off companies.
PRON is currently one of two co-directors of the MESA+ Institute at the University of Twente.
In 2002 PRON won the Simon Stevin Master Award of the Dutch Society of Science and Engineering.
In 2009 PRON won the Spinoza Prize, the most prestigious Dutch science prize.
As there was no fourth Spinoza Prize awarded in 2009, Van den NAME and PRON co-winners NAME and NAME asked the NWO to reward them the remaining prize money, which they would spend on a collaborative research effort.
Their efforts culminated in a research paper on migraine published in PLOS ONE in 2013.
The paper claimed that a critical tipping point of neurons started a migraine attack.
PRON is a member of the Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences since 2008, and its council 2011–2016.
