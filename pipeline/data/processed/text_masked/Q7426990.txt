Gluon  NAME was a key contributor to the discovery of the gluon, a particle that binds, or glues, quarks together to form protons and neutrons.
For PRON effort, NAME and PRON collaborators were awarded the 1995 European Physical Society High Energy and Particle Physics Prize.
The smoking gun signature proving the existence of the gluon were the so-called 'three jet events' occurring in electron-positron annihilation into a quark-antiquark pair, where an additional gluon is radiated from one of the quarks, creating the third jet.
In the late 1970s NAME joined the TASSO Collaboration that operated at the PETRA accelerator at DESY.
In 1979 PRON published a paper with NAME on a method of three-jet analysis in electron-positron annihilation, that was used in the following publication with the entire TASSO Collaboration, regarded as the first evidence of a gluon.
Working from first principles, NAME calculated that a collision energy of roughly 22 GeV would be needed to cleanly resolve a three-jet signature, close to PETRA's planned operating energy, and worked out with PRON postdoctoral researcher NAME how to disentangle the three overlapping jets, based on the insight that the quark, antiquark, and gluon jets must lie in a common "event plane."
When the PETRA accelerator's energy first exceeded NAME's calculated threshold in May–June 1979, NAME and NAME used their method to identify the first three-jet event recorded at PETRA.
NAME presented the result, crediting NAME and NAME, at the "Neutrino '79" conference in Bergen, Norway, in June 1979, where TASSO's three-jet observation was the only such result among the four PETRA experiments; NAME and NAME documented the finding that month in TASSO Note No.
84, and further three-jet events were presented by NAME at the European Physical Society Conference in Geneva the following month.
The other three PETRA collaborations—JADE, NAME, and PLUTO—developed comparable analyses over the following months, and all four experiments reported confirming evidence by the International Symposium on Lepton-Photon Interactions at Fermilab in August 1979.
In September 1980, using the same three-jet analysis techniques, TASSO further established that the new particle carried spin 1, as predicted for the gluon, completing its experimental identification.
