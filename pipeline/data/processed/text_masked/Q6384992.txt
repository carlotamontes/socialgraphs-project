NAME (born May 18, 1968) is an American physicist and a professor of applied physics at the California Institute of Technology (Caltech).
PRON contributions are in the areas of nanoscience, ultra-low temperature physics, and quantum effects.
Biography  After attending St. Louis University High, NAME received a Bachelor of Arts in physics from the University of Chicago in 1990 and a Ph.D. in physics from University of California, Berkeley in 1996.
PRON wrote a dissertation "Experiments with Superfluid Oscillators" under advisor NAME, where PRON demonstrated an ultra-sensitive gyroscope based on the quantum properties of superfluid helium.
PRON joined Caltech in 1996 as a Sherman Fairchild Distinguished Postdoctoral Scholar In the group of Professor NAME.
There PRON made the first observation of the quantum of thermal conductance which is the quantum mechanical limit for energy flow through single quantum channels  An electron micrograph of the nanodevice PRON designed and fabricated for this work resides in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art.
NAME joined  the U.S. National Security Agency in 2000 and led a group to study the quantum limits of mechanical structures, during which time PRON was named as a promising young innovator by Technology Review.
In 2002, NAME was named to the MIT Technology Review TR100 as one of the top 100 innovators in the world under the age of 35.
In 2005, PRON was named a Young Global Leader by the World Economic Forum and attended the annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland in 2005, 2007, and 2008.
In 2006, NAME moved to Cornell as an associate professor of physics where PRON group focused on both the cooling of mechanical structures to near the quantum ground state, and the observation of motion which fundamentally avoids the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle.
In 2009 PRON joined Caltech as a professor of applied physics.
PRON group explores the following topics: producing squeezed states of motion, exploring ultra-low dissipation superfluid resonators, ultra-sensitive microwave detection using graphene-based bolometers, and developing wide-band parametric amplifiers.
In 2014 PRON research group demonstrated the detection of motion which avoids the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle and the detection of the force noise generated by the quantum zero-point energy of a microwave field.
Recently, this group has produces a quantum squeezed state of motion, where the fluctuations of one quadrature of motion are below the quantum zero-point level.
