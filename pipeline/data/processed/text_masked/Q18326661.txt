PRON is a professor at the University of Lyon 1 and was elected knight in the Legion of Honour, in the order of the Academic palms and in the order of the Arts and Letters.

PRON conducts PRON research at the Lyon Institut de Physique des 2 Infinis (IP2I)—formerly the Institute of Nuclear Physics (IPNL)—and was co-director of two international teams (Cosmic Flows and CLUES), PRON is best known for PRON investigations into the dynamic cosmography of the Universe.
PRON work has concentrated on the distribution of matter in the large-scale structure of the Universe.
In 2006, PRON participated in the confirmation of the acceleration in the expansion of the Universe via the study of supernovae.
In 2014, PRON proposed a redefinition of the notion of galactic superclusters, and identified NAME, an agglomeration that is bigger than the Virgo Supercluster by a factor of 100.
In 2017, PRON showed that cosmic voids produce a repulsive effect resulting in galactic displacements; this may also explain the cold spot in the cosmic microwave background.
In 2023 PRON mapped the five neighbouring superclusters of Lanikea.

Career Research work Courtois began PRON research into the large-scale structure of our cosmic neighbourhood in the early 1990s while at the Lyon Observatory.
In 1994, PRON revealed a superstructure comprising 27,000 galaxies, with a radius of about 290 million light-years.
By studying the distribution of thousands of galaxies and modelling their structures, PRON team showed that these galaxies were not spherically distributed in space, but instead created a shape like a squashed football or a silkworm cocoon.
Courtois and PRON team have studied the convergent behaviour of the galaxies of the Laniakea supercluster.
By modelling the movement of galaxies as though they were streams in a watershed or basin, they were able to map out the boundaries of this local agglomeration.
They have adapted the technique to model the Vela Supercluster, which was discovered in 2016.
They used their data to compute the flow rate of the galaxies towards the 'centre', thereby making it possible to infer the masses of the galaxies.
Using this information, they will be able to determine the shape and size of the NAME supercluster.
In 2017, Courtois revealed two discoveries: one, called the NAME repeller, and the other, on the cosmic microwave background cold spot, which could potentially explain the propagation of our galaxy through space at about 2 million kilometres per hour.
In 2019, PRON team released a new map of the local universe, encompassing a volume 10 times larger than that released in 2014.
