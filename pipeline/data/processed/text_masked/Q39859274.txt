NAME  is an American natural product chemist working to understand the molecular machinery involved in assembling important plant natural products – vinblastine, morphine, iridoids, secologanin – and how changing the enzymes involved in this pathway lead to diverse analogs.
PRON was a Project Leader at the John Innes Centre in the UK between 2011 and 2019.
O'Connor was appointed by the Max Planck Society in 2018 to head the Department of Natural Product Biosynthesis at the Max Planck Institute for Chemical Ecology in Jena, Germany, taking up PRON role during 2019.
Education  O'Connor received PRON Ph.D. working with NAME on conformational effects induced by large proteins at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).
PRON was a postdoctoral fellow at Harvard Medical School, where PRON worked on epothiolone biosynthesis with Professor NAME where PRON was first introduced to plant biochemistry.
PRON later returned to MIT as a professor from 2003 to 2010, and then from 2011 to 2019 PRON was Project Leader and professor in Biological Chemistry at the John Innes Centre.
Since 2019, PRON is Director of the Department of Natural Product Biosynthesis at the Max Planck Institute for Chemical Ecology.
Research  O'Connor's work involves detailed study of many important species of medicinally-relevant plants: Rauvolfia serpentina, Catharanthus roseus, and Aspergillus japonicus.
PRON lab utilizes bioinformatics and enzyme characterization to uncover new pathways by which plants construct these molecules.
Insertion of new enzymes, for example a halogenase or oxidase results in novel variants of the molecules not found in nature.
In addition, O'Connor has started to study the molecule nepetalactone, a potential insect repellent by viewing the biosynthetic pathways of aphids with the help of etomologists.
