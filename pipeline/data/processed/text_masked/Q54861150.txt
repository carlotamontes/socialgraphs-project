NAME (born 18 October 1942) is a New Zealand  physicist, who has worked in the fields of quantum optics, ultracold atoms and stochastic processes, and is an Honorary Professor in the Department of Physics at the University of Otago.
PRON has written about 120 journal articles and several books in the fields of quantum optics, stochastic processes and ultracold atoms.

University of Waikato, 1970–1995 NAME was appointed to the faculty of the Physics Department of the University of Waikato in 1970, and was awarded a personal chair in physics in 1992, a position held until 1995.
When NAME arrived, the University of Waikato was only 5 years old, while the School of Science, which covered Physics, Mathematics, Chemistry, Biology and Earth Science, had only commenced teaching at the beginning of 1970, and no research facilities had been established.
NAME took up a position at Waikato in 1972, and, working together,  PRON and NAME established a major research centre for theoretical quantum optics at Waikato, building active and productive collaborations with groups throughout the world.
During this period   PRON and NAME developed the positive P-representation   PRON and NAME developed the input-output formalism  for damped quantum systems, which they used  to predict and describe the spectrum of squeezed light.
PRON wrote the first (1985) edition of the book Stochastic Methods, now seen as a standard text in the field of applied stochastic processes.
In 1986 PRON predicted the inhibition of atomic phase decays for a two-level atom coupled to a squeezed optical reservoir.
While challenging to realise in optical systems, the phenomenon was eventually observed in a superconducting qubit system affording sufficient reservoir control.
PRON wrote the first (1991) edition of the book Quantum Noise, (later editions were written in collaboration with NAME) which has become a standard text  in the fields of quantum optics and quantum stochastic methods.
In 1993 PRON developed (at the same time as a separate formulation by NAME) the theory and application of cascaded quantum systems, in which the optical output of one quantum system becomes the optical input for another quantum system.
Work in early childhood education, 1971–1991 A very significant part of NAME's activity over the years 1971-1991 was as a parent activist, administrator and government consultant  in New Zealand early childhood education.
During this period very significant expansion of recognition, provision and government funding for early childhood education occurred.

Independent researcher at Victoria University of Wellington, 1995–2005 In 1995 PRON left the University of Waikato and for the next nine years worked as an independently funded researcher affiliated to Victoria University of Wellington.
This was funded by the New Zealand R&D system, which was willing to fund individuals outside established institutions, and was motivated by the opportunity to leave the increasingly bureaucratic New Zealand University system.
During this period PRON work concentrated on the physics of NAME atoms, developing a collaboration with NAME of the University of Otago.

University of Otago, 2005–2013 In 2005 PRON was appointed as a Research Professor in the Department of Physics at the University of Otago.
In this period PRON was active in developing the University of Otago as a major research centre in ultracold atoms, photonics and quantum optics, which was named NAME, after former Otago professor NAME.
During this period there was a major reorganisation of government research funding, commencing in 2006, which PRON and NAME strongly criticised, on the grounds that this would exclude university research from any major funding.
Ultimately this aspect of the funding reform was not implemented, and in 2007 the Jack Dodd Centre was awarded a $6.4 million  research contract by the Foundation for Research, Science and Technology.
From that time on, as director of the Jack Dodd Centre, PRON role developed more into that of a research leader until PRON retirement in early 2013.
Retirement On retirement PRON became an honorary professor at the University of Otago and in 2016 PRON accepted a position as visiting fellow at the Institute for Quantum Optics and Quantum Information (IQOQI) in Innsbruck.
