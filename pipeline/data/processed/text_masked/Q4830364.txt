Research  Brunger is known for developing a computer program called CNS used for solving structures based on X-ray diffraction or solution NMR data, which was first released in 1992.
The program is a major extension of a 1987 program developed with NAME and Karplus called X-PLOR, whose original inspiration was motivated by Marius Clore's efforts in interpreting NMR data and which has been extended by Clore's continued development of XPLOR-NIH.
These programs make use of a method called simulated annealing in conjunction with molecular dynamics to refine protein structures.
X-PLOR was the first time a modern optimization technique was applied to the problem of crystallographic refinement.
NAME also subsequently introduced the RFree technique to cross-validate the model given the observed data.
In the mid-1990s, PRON team extended X-PLOR into a complete system to solve structures, which then became the more full-featured tool CNS, capable of performing a series of steps necessary for crystallography structure determination, such as obtaining phases from experimental data and molecular replacement phasing from known homologous structures.
