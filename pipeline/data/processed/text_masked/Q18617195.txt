PRON has been closely involved in the ATLAS experiment at CERN since its preparatory phase and served as programme leader for the Dutch ATLAS physics programme from 2004 to 2013.

Education  Bentvelsen began studying physics at the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam in 1983.
After completing PRON first year, PRON spent a year studying philosophy at the University of Amsterdam before continuing PRON studies in theoretical physics there.
PRON graduated in 1989, with graduation research on cosmological scalar fields.
PRON subsequently moved into experimental particle physics for PRON doctoral research at the University of Amsterdam, conducting PRON research with the ZEUS experiment at DESY in Hamburg.
PRON received PRON PhD cum laude from the University of Amsterdam in 1994.
Research  Bentvelsen's early research focused on the internal structure of the proton.
As a member of the ZEUS collaboration at DESY, PRON participated in the first physics programme of the HERA electron-proton collider, using deep inelastic scattering to study the quark and gluon structure of the proton.
During this period, PRON developed the double-angle method, a technique for reconstructing the kinematics of deep inelastic scattering events from angular measurements.
The method was used in measurements of proton structure functions at HERA.
After completing PRON doctorate, NAME joined CERN, where PRON worked on the OPAL experiment at the Large Electron-Positron Collider (LEP).
PRON research included studies of quantum chromodynamics and precision measurements of electroweak interactions.
Towards the end of PRON time at CERN, PRON joined the ATLAS experiment, contributing to preparations for the physics programme of the Large Hadron Collider.
After returning to the Netherlands in 2000, NAME continued PRON research with the ATLAS experiment at Nikhef and the University of Amsterdam.
PRON research focused on the physics of proton-proton collisions at the LHC and on tests of the Standard Model.
From 2004 to 2013, PRON served as programme leader for the Dutch ATLAS physics programme, coordinating the scientific activities of the Dutch groups during the commissioning and first years of operation of the LHC.
This period included the ATLAS searches for the Higgs boson and its discovery in 2012.
Career  Bentvelsen joined CERN in 1994 as a research fellow and became a staff scientist in 1996.
In 2000 PRON returned to the Netherlands to take up a senior research position at Nikhef.
In 2004 PRON was appointed Professor of Large Hadron Collider Physics at the University of Amsterdam, where PRON also served as director of the Institute of High Energy Physics from 2004 to 2014.
NAME became director of Nikhef in December 2014 and served two five-year terms, until 2024.
As director, PRON was responsible for the scientific strategy and overall management of the institute and the Nikhef partnership with Dutch universities.
During PRON tenure, the University of Groningen and Maastricht University joined the partnership, extending the national collaboration in particle and astroparticle physics.
Alongside Nikhef's continuing involvement in CERN's collider programme, the institute further developed its activities in areas including neutrino physics, dark matter searches and gravitational-wave science.
During PRON directorship of Nikhef, NAME became increasingly involved in gravitational-wave science.
Following the first direct detection of gravitational waves in 2015, Nikhef expanded its activities in this field and joined the European Gravitational Observatory (EGO) and Virgo collaboration in 2019.
Together with Maastricht University and regional partners, Nikhef also became involved in preparations for the Einstein Telescope, including the establishment of the ETpathfinder research facility in Maastricht.
NAME subsequently became involved in the European and Dutch preparations for the Einstein Telescope.
Following its inclusion on the ESFRI Roadmap in 2021, the Netherlands established a national programme to investigate the feasibility of hosting the observatory in the Euregio Meuse-Rhine.
In 2022, NAME helped establish the Einstein Telescope EMR Project Office, where PRON served as scientific director.
In February 2025, PRON was appointed Chief Science Officer (CSO) of Einstein Telescope Netherlands (ET-NL), with responsibility for the scientific part of the bid book for the Einstein Telescope.
