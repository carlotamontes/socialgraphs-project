Dr. NAME (born in 1974), a Venezuelan chemist, is the Brush Family Professor of Chemistry at the University of Pennsylvania.
PRON specializes in inorganic and organometallic synthesis, catalysis, and mechanistic chemistry.

Early life and career NAME was born in 1974 in San Cristóbal, Táchira, Venezuela.
PRON learned English by attending a bilingual school until third grade.
In 1989, PRON immigrated to the United States, where PRON completed PRON high school studies graduating 1992.
PRON attended Michigan State University and graduated with a Bachelor of Science in chemistry with honors in 1996.
As an undergraduate, PRON conducted research with NAME, studying the binding modes of purine bases to anti-tumor drugs containing Re, Rh, and Pt.
In 2000, PRON completed PRON PhD with NAME at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology studying atom and group transfer reactions and low coordinate group 5 and 6 transition metal complexes.
PRON then worked as a NIH and FORD Postdoctoral Research Fellow for the late NAME at the University of Chicago on metal mediated nitrous oxide reductions and group 10 complexes with multiple metal-ligand bonds.
PRON joined the faculty in the department of chemistry at Indiana University in 2002, where PRON was promoted to associate professor in 2007 and professor in 2011.
In 2013, PRON moved to the University of Pennsylvania as the Presidential Chair Professor of Chemistry before being named the Brush Family Professor of Chemistry in 2018.
PRON served as an associate editor for Dalton Transactions (2011–2014) and is currently an associate editor for Organometallics (2014–present).
PRON has been a member of the editorial board for Inorganic Syntheses since 2014.
Research interests PRON research interests within organometallic chemistry focus on the synthesis of highly reactive transition metal species including radicals, complexes with metal-ligand multiple bonds, and the reactivity of such species with small molecules.
C-H activation reactions  Much of Mindiola's work with transition metal complexes has been applied to the intermolecular activation of inert C-H bonds to convert hydrocarbons into industrial products in a safe and cost-effective manner.
Notably, in 2016, PRON team was able to catalytically borylate methane with an iridium catalyst.
Complexes with multiple metal-ligand bonds  Another thrust of PRON research program is the synthesis of coordinately unsaturated transition metal complexes with multiple metal-ligand bonds, especially metal alkylidene and alkylidyne complexes.
These complexes can act as catalysts in olefin metathesis, polymerization, and group transfer reactions.
In an account, PRON describes the synthesis of highly reactive titanium and vanadium species that were previously considered to be too unstable.
