Nam-Gyu Park (; born 3 September 1960) is a South Korean chemical engineer.
PRON is Distinguished Professor and Sungkyunkwan University (SKKU)-Fellow at School of Chemical Engineering, SKKU.
PRON research focuses on high efficiency mesoscopic nanostructured solar cells.
PRON announced the beginning of perovskite photovoltaic research by first reporting long-term stable perovskite solar cells in 2012, and is called the pioneer of practical perovskite solar cells.
Career PRON is from Masan, South Gyeongsang Province (now Changwon).
When PRON was young, PRON parent was transferred to the NAME branch of Korea Electric Power Corporation, so PRON moved to NAME and received PRON elementary, middle, and high school education there.
As a teenager, PRON wanted to be an artist or architect, but PRON couldn't due to opposition from PRON music teacher.
Upon entering the Department of Chemistry Education at Seoul National University in 1981, PRON joined the 9th Infantry Division (also known as the White Horse Division) and served for two years and seven months.
PRON returned to Seoul National University after being discharged from the military in 1984, majored in inorganic chemistry, and graduated with a bachelor's degree in 1988.
Since January 1988, PRON worked as a researcher at SKC (formerly known as Sunkyung Chemical)'s Cheonan plant, which was then well-known as a videotape, compact disc and floppy disk manufacturer, but PRON resigned in February 1990.
PRON then attended the Department of Chemistry at Seoul National University and became a student of Professor NAME.
At first, PRON wanted to study superconductors, but changed PRON direction to perovskite research at the suggestion of the professor.
In February 1992, PRON earned PRON master's degree in inorganic chemistry from Seoul National University graduate school, and in February 1995, PRON earned PRON doctorate in inorganic chemistry through a paper titled "Synthesis and physico-chemical properties of 2-dimensional inorganic solids and their intercalation compounds".
From March 1996 to May 1997, PRON worked as a postdoctoral researcher at the Institut de Chimie de la Matiere Condensee de Bordeaux – Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique (ICMCB-CNRS) in Pessac, France, and conducted research to improve efficiency of dye-sensitized solar cells.
From June 1997 to December 1999, PRON worked as a postdoctoral researcher at the National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) in Golden, Colorado, the United States.
After returning to South Korea in January 2000, PRON worked as a senior researcher at the Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute (ETRI), and moved to the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST) in December 2005 as the head of the solar cell research center and the head of the energy materials research center.
However, dye-sensitized solar cells have some disadvantages in that they are economical but have limited energy conversion efficiency.
Meanwhile, at a solar cell-related conference held in Switzerland in 2007, NAME, a professor at Toin University of Yokohama, Japan, presented a presentation on perovskite solar cells.
In a paper published in 2009, NAME first presented a dye-sensitized solar cell model with perovskite structural materials, but it was implemented in a liquid state, making it less stable and only 3.8% efficient, so it received little attention.
When PRON was appointed as a professor at Sungkyunkwan University in July 2009, PRON conducted research on perovskites, and paid attention to the fact that the perovskite structural compounds have superconductivity along with properties of nonconductors, semiconductors, and conductors and thus have properties that absorb light well.
In August 2012, PRON developed the world's first solar cell made of solid perovskite with Professor NAME at the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne, Switzerland.
The solid perovskite solar cell PRON developed recorded the highest efficiency at the time of 9.7%, and the efficiency remained constant even when exposed to the outside for more than 500 hours.
Published in the journal Scientific Reports, a sibling paper of Nature, it was considered the first paper on a stable and efficient solid perovskite solar cell.
Since then, it has been evaluated as providing an opportunity to conduct research on perovskite solar cells around the world.
PRON perovskite solar cell research achievements gained international recognition by winning the British Rank Prizes for Optoelectronics in 2022, the Italian ENI award for Energy Frontiers in 2024, and the German Humboldt Research Award in 2025.
In 2023, PRON was the only South Korean scientists and technicians to be invited to the Nobel Symposium hosted by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences to present solid perovskite solar cells.
