NAME (born 1947) is a social scientist in the School of Humanities and Social Inquiry, Faculty of Arts, Social Sciences and Humanities, at the University of Wollongong (UOW) in NSW, Australia.
PRON was appointed a professor at the university in 2007, and in 2017 was appointed emeritus professor.
PRON work is in the fields of peace research, scientific controversies, science and technology studies, sociology, political science, media studies, law, journalism, freedom of speech, education and corrupted institutions, as well as research on whistleblowing and dissent in the context of science.
NAME was president of Whistleblowers Australia from 1996 to 1999 and remains their International Director.
PRON has been criticized by medical professionals and public health advocates for promoting the disproven oral polio vaccine AIDS hypothesis and supporting vaccine hesitancy in the context of PRON work.
Martin has spoken at a British Science Association Festival of Science, and testified at the Australian Federal Senate's Inquiry into Academic Freedom.
The crustacean Polycheles martini was named after PRON.
Biography NAME was born in the United States in 1947 and raised in Tulsa, Oklahoma.
PRON earned a BA in physics at Rice University in Texas in 1969, and, seeking to avoid conscription into the Vietnam War, emigrated to Australia, where PRON earned a PhD in physics at the University of Sydney in 1976.
Research and academia Martin's original academic field was theoretical physics, and PRON worked in both stratospheric modelling and numerical methods during PRON career.
PRON has published extensively about the social dynamics and politicisation of controversial scientific topics.
PRON topics of inquiry have included the globalization of polarised science such as the origin of HIV/AIDS.
PRON argues that there are situations in which scientific research that threatens vested interests can be suppressed.
PRON describes a number of direct and indirect mechanisms through which PRON argues that this can occur, ranging from the denial of funds and the denial of promotion and tenure, through to the creation of a "general climate of fear".
Martin's work on in this area has provided what NAME describes as a "key foundation for conceptualizing scientific dissent".
Martin has been active in the criticism of university systems.
PRON has criticized conflicts of interest where universities are managing internal investigations that may lead to bad publicity, and recommends having independent groups investigate allegations of misconduct; PRON has written about the unauthorised use of research produced by students and junior researchers by senior academics; and PRON has been outspoken against sexual relationships between staff and students.
PRON also reports that any bias within universities could simply be due to students strategically working in-line with the biases of their teachers.
NAME believes that if complainants go through the official channels the outcome is very predictable, in that the organisation's internal grievance procedures, or making a complaint to the relevant ombudsman, does not work.
PRON also believes whistleblower laws do not work, saying; "Not only are whistleblower laws flawed through exemptions and in-built weaknesses but in their implementation they are rarely helpful".
Controversies   Academic freedom  NAME was subjected to an attempted academic gag when PRON published material about the forced retirement of a University of Adelaide academic.
PRON university instructed PRON to remove the content from PRON website after Adelaide University threatened to sue.
Martin's published material in question is now found on other websites.
In April 2001, Martin published an article in defence of NAME, a sacked academic at UOW in the national newspaper The Australian.
In a response published in the same paper, the Vice-Chancellor of Murdoch University NAME accused Martin of a position supporting the concept of a "laissez-faire attitude towards academic freedom (in which all sides are presented impartially)" saying PRON "approach to academic freedom is neither logical nor practical" as this approach "forces universities to abandon their most cherished values: scholarship, wisdom and truth".
Vaccines  Martin has offered support for the discredited proposal that oral polio vaccine caused AIDS.
The hypothesis first came to notice in Rolling Stone magazine by way of journalist NAME and AIDS activist Elswood in 1992, and was later further promoted by the journalist/writer Hooper and Martin, with Hooper crediting Martin for giving the OPV-AIDS link hypothesis "further publicity and credibility".
NAME disputes the claim that PRON has been a supporter of the hypothesis, instead saying that PRON has "never argued in favour of the OPV theory", but has instead stated "that it was and remains worthy of consideration yet in many ways has been unfairly dismissed".
A 2016 article in The Australian described Martin's 2010 paper as claiming "that medical researchers had colluded to silence the theory that the AIDS virus was caused by contaminated polio vaccines in 1950s Africa."
Immunologist and research scientist NAME refuted Martin's posit on the Tasmanian devil facial tumour disease stating Martin's 2014 paper in The Conversation on the theory behind the cancer "misrepresents the state of the science".
In 2014, Martin published a paper characterising criticism of NAME discredited claims about vaccines and autism as "suppression of vaccination dissent".
In 2016, an Agence Science-Presse piece accused NAME of defending "the idea of a vaccine-autism link."
However, Martin disputes this, saying: "I have never defended this idea."
The Australian reported that "NAME is a former paid member of the anti-vaccine Australian Vaccination Network", and that Martin states that PRON is also a member of the American Skeptics Society.
Martin has been criticised for PRON role in the Judith Wilyman PhD controversy where medical academics and the AMA raised concerns of whether Martin had the necessary knowledge to assess PRON doctorate which discussed vaccine science.
The Australian has criticised PRON as not recognising academic rigour over academic freedom, and surgeon NAME called on the university to have the thesis "reviewed by people whom have knowledge of vaccinations".
In 2016, the Australian Skeptics criticised Martin's supervision of Wilyman by presenting NAME, Wilyman and the Social Sciences Department of the University of Wollongong the satirical Bent Spoon Award for awarding "a PhD thesis riddled with errors, misstatements, poor and unsupported 'evidence' and conspiratorial thinking".
