Research summary  NAME's graduate research at Berkeley was on the parity non-conserving neutral weak interaction in atomic thallium.
PRON co-authored a textbook on the larger subject of electroweak interactions after completing PRON doctoral thesis.
At Bell Laboratories PRON became interested in ultrafast and strong field laser-matter interactions.
For a time, PRON co-held the record for the shortest wavelength coherent radiation produced in the laboratory.
PRON was one of the team that used similar methods to develop  the first ultrafast angle-resolved vuv photoemission methods.
In 1985 PRON turned to the study of strong-field ionization of atoms.
PRON early work on above threshold ionization of atoms established the role of ponderomotive forces in laser-electron interactions through studies of electron surfing in ultrafast laser pulses as well as the high-intensity Kapitsa–Dirac effect.
PRON also discovered and explained the mechanism of bond softening in strong-field molecular dissociation.
PRON pioneering development of broadband coherent THz radiation (so-called "half-cycle pulses") helped to advance the field of ultrafast THz spectroscopy.
PRON has subsequently used ultrafast lasers to study problems in quantum sculpting, quantum information, and coherent control of atomic and molecular dynamics.
NAME helped to establish the new field of ultrafast x-ray science in early work at the Advanced Photon Source at Argonne National Laboratory and most recently strong-field coherent x-ray-atom physics at x-ray free-electron lasers.
