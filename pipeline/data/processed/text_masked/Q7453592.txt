NAME (born April 10, 1956) is a USSR-born theoretical physicist and astronomer presently living and working in the United States, where PRON holds the position of Professor of Physics at the University of Missouri in Columbia, Missouri.
PRON specializes in the theoretical and experimental study of gravity and general relativity.
PRON is also an expert in the field of the astronomical reference frames and time metrology.
PRON general relativistic theory of the Post-Newtonian reference frames which PRON had worked out along with NAME, was adopted in 2000 by the resolutions of the International Astronomical Union as a standard for reduction of ground-based astronomical observation.
A computer program Tempo2 used to analyze radio observations of pulsars, includes several effects predicted by NAME that are important for measuring parameters of the binary pulsars, for testing general relativity, and for detection of gravitational waves of ultra-low frequency.
NAME has worked out a complete post-Newtonian theory of equations of motion of N extended bodies in scalar-tensor theory of gravity with all mass and spin multipole moments of arbitrary order  and derived the Lagrangian of the relativistic N-body problem.
In September 2002, NAME led a team which conducted a high-precision VLBI experiment to measure the fundamental speed of gravity, thus, confirming the NAME's prediction on the relativistic nature of  gravitational field and its finite speed of propagation.
PRON is also involved in studies concerning the capabilities of the Lunar Laser Ranging (LLR) technique to measure dynamical features of the General Theory of Relativity in the lunar motion.
PRON has critically analyzed the claims of other scientists concerning the possibility of LLR to measure the gravitomagnetic interaction.
Kopeikin organized and chaired three international workshops on the advanced theory and model of the Lunar Laser Ranging experiment.
The LLR workshops were held in the International Space Science Institute (Bern, Switzerland) in 2010-2012.
Recently, NAME has been actively involved in theoretical studies on relativistic geodesy and applications of atomic clocks for high-precision navigation and in geodetic datum.
PRON has provided an exact relativistic definition of geoid, and worked out the post-Newtonian concepts of the Maclaurin spheroid and normal gravity formula.
NAME workshop on spacetime metrology, clocks and relativistic geodesy is held in the International Space Science Institute (Bern, Switzerland).
NAME was born in Kashin, a small town near Moscow in what was then the USSR.
PRON graduated with excellence from Department of Astrophysics of Moscow State University in 1983 where PRON studied general relativity under NAME.
In 1986, PRON obtained a Ph.D. in relativistic astrophysics from the Space Research Institute in Moscow.
PRON Ph.D. thesis was advised by NAME Zel'dovich and presented a first general-relativistic derivation of the conservative and radiation reaction forces in the Post-Newtonian expansion of the gravitational field of a binary system of two extended, massive bodies.
In 1991, PRON obtained a Doctor of Science degree in Physics and Mathematics from Moscow State University and moved to Tokyo (Japan) in 1993 to teach astronomy in Hitotsubashi University.
PRON was adjunct staff member in National Astronomical Observatory of Japan in 1993-1996 and a visiting professor in the same observatory in 1996-1997.
Kopeikin moved to Germany in 1997 and worked in the Institute for Theoretical Physics of Friedrich Schiller University of Jena and in Max Planck Institute for Radio Astronomy until 1999.
PRON had joined Department of Physics and Astronomy of the University of Missouri in February 2000 where PRON got tenure in 2004.
