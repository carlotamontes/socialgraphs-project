Principal research contributions In 1978, NAME was appointed as a head of the Laboratory of Biophysical Introscopy of the Institute of Chemical Physics of the Russian Academy of Sciences.
In the early 1980s, a high-resolution (up to approximately 500 MHz) transmission-mode SAM, scanning acoustic microscopy, system was developed at the Laboratory of Biophysical Introscopy of the Russian Academy of Sciences under NAME and PRON research group, contributing to early developments in acoustic imaging techniques.
In 1980, NAME, together with A. Atalar (Stanford Univ.)
were invited as consultants to NAME group at the Ernst Leitz Wetzlar, GmbH (Germany) to assist in the development of an early commercial SAM system, ELSAM, with the broad frequency range from 100 MHz up to ultra high 1.8 GHz.
From 1984 to 1990, NAME with the colleagues from PRON lab developed the theory to determine the amplitude of acoustic waves occurring in transmission-mode acoustic microscopy and derived new quantitative amplitude-based methods for more accurate material characterization.
During the same period PRON lab developed portable SAM systems which was implemented in a few research institutions in Russia, Ukraine, Latvia, China and Germany.
Later, in 2001, NAME developed a handheld high-frequency (up to 100 MHz) ultrasonic imaging system for the characterization of subsurface and bulk structures of advanced materials, such as metals and alloys, ceramics, composites and polymers and, recently, for subsurface imaging of hard and soft tissues.
Over the course of PRON career, NAME has established an Acoustic Microscopy Center in Moscow and the Institute for Diagnostic Imaging Research in Windsor, Ontario.
