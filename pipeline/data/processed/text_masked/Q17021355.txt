Jean-Claude Bradley was a chemist who actively promoted Open Science in chemistry, including at the White House, for which PRON was awarded the Blue Obelisk award in 2007.
PRON coined the term "Open Notebook science".
PRON died in May 2014.
A memorial symposium was held July 14, 2014 at Cambridge University, UK.
One outcome of PRON Open Notebook work is the collection of physicochemical properties of organic compounds PRON was studying.
All of this data PRON made available as Open data under the CCZero license.
For example, in 2009 Bradley et al.
published their work on making solubility data of organic compounds available as Open data.
Later, the melting point data set PRON collaborated on with NAME and NAME was published with Figshare.
Both data sets were also made available as books via the Lulu.com self-publishing platform.
PRON blogged extensively and contributed to at least 25 individual blogs.
In an interview in 2008 with NAME titled "Doing Science Publicly", PRON spoke of PRON work and online presence.
In 2010, PRON gave an extensive interview about the impact of Open Notebook science with NAME.
