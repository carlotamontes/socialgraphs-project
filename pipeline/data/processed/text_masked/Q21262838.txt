PRON research considers the electronic and optical properties of carbon nanomaterials.
Early life and education  Maultzsch was an undergraduate student at Technische Universität Berlin, where PRON studied physics.
PRON remained in Berlin for doctoral research, investigating the vibrational properties of carbon allotropes including nanotubes and graphite.
Research and career  Maultzsch moved to the University of Siegen, where PRON worked on near field microscopy.
PRON returned to Technische Universität Berlin in late 2004, where PRON worked as a researcher in the Institute of Solid State Physics.
PRON moved to Columbia University in 2006, working as a postdoctoral researcher with NAME.
PRON returned Technische Universität Berlin in 2008, where PRON was made an assistant professor.
PRON is interested in low-dimensional materials, including transition-metal dichalcogenides and graphene.
Maultzsch contributed to the understanding of carbon nanotubes.
In particular, PRON investigated their electronic and vibrational properties.
PRON made use of resonant Raman spectroscopy to uncover the optical transition energies and radial mode frequencies of various metallic and semiconducting nanotubes.
PRON showed that the optical transition energies and electronic structures depend on the chirality index of the nanotube.
PRON revealed that electron-phonon coupling increases for smaller chiral angles.
In 2010, Maultzsch was awarded an ERC Starting Grant focused on the characterisation of carbon nanomaterials.
In 2015, PRON was promoted to full Professor for Novel Materials.
In 2017, Maultzsch was appointed the Chair of Experimental Physics at the University of Erlangen–Nuremberg.
