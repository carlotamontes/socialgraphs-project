María Ángela Nieto Toledano (Madrid, March 1, 1960) is a Spanish scientist, doctor in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology.
PRON is recognized for PRON groundbreaking work in the fields of developmental biology and cellular biology.
PRON works at the Institute for Neurosciences CSIC-UMH, a joint center of the Spanish National Research Council (CSIC) and the Miguel Hernández University of Elche (UMH).
Career  PRON received PRON doctorate from the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid in 1987 for PRON work on interactions of nucleic acid proteins.
In 1988 PRON worked at the  Instituto de Investigaciones Biomédicas in Madrid, to study programmed cell death.
In 1989, PRON joined the National Institute for Medical Research in London, working with NAME where PRON isolated a number of genes involved in the morphogenesis of the nervous system.
In 1993 In 1993, PRON obtained a position of Staff Scientist at the Cajal Institute in Madrid.
Since then PRON has led a research group interested in cell movements during embryo development and adult pathologies.
In 2000, PRON was promoted to director of the Developmental Neurobiology Department and elected member of the European Molecular Biology Organization.
In 2004 Nieto was promoted to research professor.
That same year, PRON moved to the Institute for Neurosciences (CSIC-UMH) in Alicante.
Nieto's has done research on epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), a process where epithelial cells, which are tightly connected and stationary, transition into mesenchymal cells, which are more mobile and invasive.
This phenomenon is critical in understanding developmental processes, as well as in cancer metastasis, tissue regeneration and disease in adults.
PRON research on the SNAI1 gene family, its activation and relationship with disease has been well received, including its involvement in the formation of different tissues and organs during embryonic development, and how its reactivation in later developmental stages or in adulthood can result in various illness, such as tumor progression, fibrosis, and bone disease.
Nieto's contributions have been fundamental in explaining how cells change their identity during development, tissue repair, and in diseases like cancer.
PRON work has helped clarify the role of EMT in the progression of various cancers, making PRON a key figure in cancer biology research.
EMT was initially accepted by developmental biologists but not by oncologists.
