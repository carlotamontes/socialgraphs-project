Research   Immunology  Tonegawa's Nobel Prize work elucidated the genetic mechanism of the adaptive immune system, which had been the central question of immunology for over 100 years.
Prior to Tonegawa's discovery, one early idea to explain the adaptive immune system suggested that each gene produces one protein; however, there are under 19,000 genes in the human body which nonetheless can produce millions of antibodies.
In experiments beginning in 1976, Tonegawa showed that genetic material rearranges itself to form millions of antibodies.

This process is known as V(D)J recombination.
