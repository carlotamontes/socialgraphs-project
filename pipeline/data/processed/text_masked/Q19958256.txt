NAME (born July 28, 1969, Hemer/NW, Germany) is a German-Finnish researcher in the field of Biochemistry.
PRON is a professor at the University of Helsinki, Finland.
PRON has more than 70 publications.
NAME was the first to show that VEGF-C and VEGF-D are the principal growth factors for the lymphatic vasculature and PRON research focuses on cancer drug targets and lymphangiogenesis.
PRON has also contributed to other seminal publications in cell biology with transgenesis, protein engineering, recombinant production and purification.
In 2006, PRON developed a synthetic super-VEGF, using a library of VEGF hybrid molecules using a novel, non-random DNA family shuffling method.
NAME completed PRON graduation, postgraduation, and postdoc at Molecular/Cancer Biology Lab, University of Helsinki, Helsinki, Finland.
PRON worked as a researcher at Lymphatix Ltd. (now Ark Therapeutics) via Licentia Ltd., Helsinki, Finland (April 2006 – January 2007) and at Circadian Technologies via Vegenics Ltd. via Licentia Ltd., Helsinki, Finland (February 2007 – December 2011), where PRON was involved in the research on VEGF-C and VEGF-D as drugs and drug targets.
PRON worked as a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Wihuri Research Institute, Helsinki, Finland from January 2013 to August 2013.
Since 2013, PRON is a Group leader and since 2020 Associate Professor for Pharmaceutical Protein Drug Research at the Faculty of Pharmacy at University of Helsinki, Helsinki, Finland.
The NAME lab is affiliated with the Drug Development Program and the Individualized Drug Therapy Research Program.
PRON was awarded NAME by the Minerva Foundation Institute for Medical Research for best biomedical publication of the year in Finland in March 1997.
In 2003, PRON received the Mandatum Prize for the best PhD thesis in the field of biotechnology in Finland.
In 2015, PRON received the “Best Paper Award” in the category of Basic Science by Circulation, the journal of the American Heart Association for PRON 2014 work, in which PRON identified the molecular mechanism behind Hennekam syndrome.
