NAME is an American physicist, working mostly in the field of general relativity and cosmology, including numerical relativity, kinetic theory, black hole physics, and gravitational radiation.
PRON is Professor of Physics at the University of Texas at Austin where PRON directed the Center for Relativity.
In 1993 PRON organized and was Lead Principal Investigator of an NSF/ARPA funded computational Grand Challenge program involving ten university teams seeking computational descriptions for the interaction of black holes as potential sources for observable gravitational radiation.
PRON work leading what became known as the Binary Black Hole Grand Challenge Alliance featured in NAME Nobel Prize lecture, including when Matzner and Alliance collaborators wagered Thorne that numerical relativity would produce a simulated waveform comparable to observation prior to the first LIGO detection.
Matzner and colleagues eventually won, NAME saying PRON "conceded the bet with great happiness."
Education and career Matzner received PRON Ph.D. from the University of Maryland in Physics in 1967, advised by NAME.
PRON concluded PRON graduate education with a year at the University of Cambridge.
Matzner has advised and educated students in cosmology, relativity, and astrophysics.
PRON was one of the first to calculate what a simplified, head-on collision between two black holes would look like, including the weak gravitational waves such a merger could emit.
PRON was elected a Fellow of the American Physical Society in 1995 for "PRON analyses in general relativity of a wide range of astrophysical phenomena, especially PRON numerical simulations of strong-field gravitational systems and the gravitational radiation they produce."
Matzner's research in theoretical questions includes topology and signature change in the early universe, and quantum gravity using the tool of path integration.
PRON observational/experimental studies include tests of General Relativistic effects via laser-ranging to dense geodetic satellites.
These experiments resulted in the first (and best as of 2023) measurement and confirmation of the NAME-Thirring frame-dragging effect, predicted by NAME's theory of general relativity, to within 10 percent.
Matzner is Co-I of the LARES (Laser Relativity Satellite) satellite team, the densest artificial satellite in orbit, launched in 2012, with a second satellite, NAME, launched in 2022.
