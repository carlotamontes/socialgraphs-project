NAME(born 22 May 1946) is a British physicist and a pioneer of string theory.
PRON is a professor of theoretical physics in the School of Physics and Astronomy at Queen Mary University of London, emeritus professor in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics and a fellow of Clare Hall, Cambridge.
PRON was the Lucasian professor of mathematics from 2009 to 2015.
Early life and education NAME was born the child of Genia Green and NAME.
PRON attended William Ellis School in London and Churchill College, Cambridge where PRON graduated with a Bachelor of Arts with first class honours in theoretical physics (1967) and a PhD in elementary particle theory (1970).
Career Following PRON PhD, Green did postdoctoral research at Princeton University (1970–72), Cambridge and the University of Oxford.
Between 1978 and 1993 PRON was a Lecturer and Professor at Queen Mary College, University of London, and in July 1993 PRON was appointed NAME Plummer Professor of Theoretical Physics at the University of Cambridge.
On 19 October 2009 PRON was confirmed as the next Lucasian Professor of Mathematics, to succeed NAME on 1 November 2009.
In 2015 PRON was succeeded in that chair by NAME, a specialist in colloids, gels, and particulate materials.
Research After many years in collaboration with NAME, PRON co-discovered type II string theory in 1982, and later the anomaly cancellation in type I string theory in 1984.
This latter insight, named the Green–Schwarz mechanism, initiated the First Superstring Revolution.
Green has also worked on Dirichlet boundary conditions in string theory which have led to the postulation of D-branes and instantons.
Awards and honours Green has been awarded the Paul Dirac and Maxwell Medals of the Institute of Physics, UK, the Dirac Medal of the International Centre for Theoretical Physics (Trieste) and the Dannie Heineman Prize for Mathematical Physics of the American Physical Society.
PRON was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1989.
Green has co-authored more than 150 research papers.
