NAME   (born 1950 in Ealing, West London) is a British-Canadian physicist, specializing in particle physics.
Education and career Pinfold graduated in physics in 1972 with a B.Sc.
from Imperial College London and in 1977 with a Ph.D. from the University of London.
PRON Ph.D. thesis was on weak neutral currents, stemming from PRON work as part of the Gargamelle discovery team.
From 1977 to 1989, PRON held research assistant and senior research assistant positions at CERN (near Geneva) and NAME (near Chicago).
From 1989 to 1992, PRON was an associate professor at the Weizmann Institute of Science.
At the University of Alberta, PRON was from 1992 to 1996 an associate professor and from 1996 to 2016 a full professor, and PRON is since 2016 a distinguished university professor.
From 1995 to 2004 PRON was the University of Alberta's Centre for Subatomic Research (renamed in 2006 the Centre for Particle Physics).
Since 2005 PRON has held a visiting professorship at King's College London.
PRON frequently travels back and forth between the University of Alberta and CERN in Geneva.
PRON is the author or co-author of over 1250 citable publications and has given over 220 invited talks.
Pinfold was from 1988 to 1989 the spokesperson for CERN's WA88 experiment.
From 1987 to 1992 PRON was the spokesperson for the MODAL experiment at CERN's Large Electron Positron Collider (LEP).
PRON was one of the founders in the 1990s of the ATLAS experiment involved in the Large NAME (LHC) discovery of the Higgs boson.
From 2000 to 2002 PRON was the deputy spokesperson for ATLAS-Canada.
Since 2000 PRON is the leader and spokesperson for the MoEDAL experiment.
From 2004 to 2010 PRON was the deputy co-spokesperson for the SLIM experiment.
In 2007, PRON won an award from ASTech (Alberta Science & Technology Leadership Foundation) for PRON leadership in starting the Alberta Large-area Time-coincidence Array, or ALTA, Project.
This educational and research project "involves spreading out many cosmic-ray detectors over vast areas, connecting them through the Internet, and synchronising their readings with an integrated GPS system.
Most of the detectors are run by high school students".
In 2013 PRON was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada (RSC).
In 2025, PRON was a recipient of the Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics as a member of the ATLAS Collaboration.
