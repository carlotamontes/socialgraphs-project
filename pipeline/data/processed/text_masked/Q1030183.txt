NAME (born December 1, 1959) is an American physicist.
PRON is a professor in the MIT Program in Media Arts and Sciences and the director of the MIT Center for Bits and Atoms.
PRON works mainly on interdisciplinary topics in physics and computer science, such as quantum computing, nanotechnology, and personal fabrication.
PRON has been called the "intellectual parent of the maker movement".
Early life and education NAME was born on December 1, 1959 in Ardmore, Pennsylvania to a Jewish family.
PRON parent, NAME, was a labor arbitrator and mediator and emeritus professor of management at Temple University, and PRON parent NAME who was also an arbitrator.
PRON attended Plymouth-Whitemarsh High School in Plymouth Meeting, Pennsylvania.
Later PRON attended Swarthmore College, graduating in 1981 with a B.A. degree in physics with high honors.

PRON received an honorary doctorate from Swarthmore College in 2006.
In 2012 PRON received an honorary doctorate from Strathclyde University.
Career  In 1998, NAME started a class at MIT called "How to make (almost) anything".
NAME wanted to introduce expensive, industrial-size machines to the technical students.
However, this class attracted a lot of students from various backgrounds: artists, architects, designers, students without any technical background.
In PRON interview to CNN, Gershenfeld said that "the students... were answering a question I didn't ask, which is: What is this stuff good for?
And the answer is: Not to make what you can buy in stores, but to make what you can't buy in stores.
It's to personalise fabrication".
NAME believes that this is the beginning of a new revolution: digital revolution in fabrication that will allow people to fabricate things, machines on demand.
NAME has presented PRON course on "How to make (almost) anything" at the Association of Professional Model Makers (APMM) 2010 Conference.
In 2023, PRON course won the Irwin Sizer Award for the Most Significant Improvement to MIT education.
This class later has led NAME to create Fab lab.
NAME feels very passionate about this project, as PRON believes that teaching kids how to use technology and create it themselves will empower the future generations to become more independent and create technology that each individual community needs, not a technology that is currently available on the market.
Fab labs exist in over 150 countries.
In PRON interview with Discover magazine on the question what personal fabrication might be useful for, NAME said, "There is a surprising need for emergent technologies in many of the least developed places on the planet.
While our needs might be fairly well met, there are billions of people on the planet whose needs are not.
Their problems don't need incremental tweaks in current technology, but a revolution".
NAME chairs Fab Foundation, leads the Fab Academy, which grew into the "Academany" platform for distributed hands-on education.
In addition to the "How to make (almost) anything" course, NAME has taught "How To Make Something That Makes (almost) Anything", "The Physics of Information Technology", "The Nature of Mathematical Modeling".
PRON was involved in starting the Interspecies Internet where PRON is a Founding Trustee.
NAME has spoken at TED conferences, and presented at the World Economic Forum in Davos.
Quantum computing pioneer In 1998, NAME and associates built the first quantum computer (2-qubit) that could be loaded with data and output a solution.
Although impractical, it demonstrated the principles of quantum computation, it was the first significant faster-than-classical quantum computation.
Ultralight material In 2013, NAME and fellow researchers at MIT introduced reversibly assembled cellular composite materials.
Other research   PRON work has been published in Science, Nature, IEEE, ACM, Cell, and many other journals, as well as in The American Physical Society journal.
NAME published an early paper on what became known as the Internet of Things.
PRON paper "Microfluidic bubble logic" introduced digital logic in microfluidics.
PRON paper on "Physical one-way functions" introduced the concept of physical one-way functions.
NAME released the Great Invention Kit in 2008, a construction set that users can manipulate to create various objects.
