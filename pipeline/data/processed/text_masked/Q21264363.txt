Marileen Dogterom (born 20 November 1967, in Utrecht) is a Dutch biophysicist and professor at the Kavli Institute of Nanoscience at Delft University of Technology.
PRON is notable for PRON research of the cell cytoskeleton.
PRON was awarded the 2018 Spinoza Prize for PRON research.
Life and career  Dogterom was born 20 November 1967 in Utrecht, the Netherlands.
In 1990, PRON earned a degree in theoretical physics from the University of Groningen.
Two years later, the Fulbright Program granted PRON a fellowship.
Though PRON found a PhD position at the University of Paris-Sud, PRON followed PRON mentor (NAME) to Princeton University, where Dogterom began to work with biological systems via a collaboration with their biology faculty.
In 1994, PRON graduated cum laude with a doctorate from the University of Paris-Sud, with a thesis on "Physical Aspects of Microtubule Growth and Mitotic Spindle Formation," and worked as a postdoc at Bell Labs in the United States.
In 1997, PRON became a project leader at FOM Institute AMOLF, a physics and biophysics research institute, becoming head of department from 2003 to 2013.
Dogterom holds professional ties to various universities.
In 2000, PRON became a professor at Leiden University, and become a full professor in 2010.
PRON was the Chair of the Department of Bionanoscience at the Kavli Institute of Nanoscience at Delft University of Technology from 2014 to 2021.
Since 2016, PRON has held the title of "Medical Delta Professor," meaning PRON held professorships at both Delft and Leiden.
In 2006, Dogterom took a sabbatical for a year to work at Erasmus MC in Anna Akhmanova's research group, which specialized in cell biology and cytoskeletons.
In 2013, they received an ERC Synergy grant to collaborate on research on cytoskeleton regulation by the cell.
Dogterom currently leads a team that aims to build the first artificial cell.
In PRON laboratory, PRON team studies and builds parts of the cytoskeleton outside of the cell, where they can measure the forces they exert on various other cell components.
The team also analyses the effects of various forces on the deformation of the cytoskeleton, the interactions of the cytoskeleton polymers as a network, and the spatial structure of the cell itself.
PRON research group plans to collaborate with various other research groups that focus on other parts of the cell.
