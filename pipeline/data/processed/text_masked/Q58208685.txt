NAME is a Ukrainian atmosphere scientist and a professor of physics and mathematics at Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv (KNU).
PRON is a professor at the International Center for Future Science within the College of Physics at Jilin University (JLU) and holds a position as principal scientist at the Main Astronomical Observatory of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine (NAS).
NAME's primary research interest lies in atmospheric studies, particularly focusing on climate change processes and their effects on ecosystems.
PRON has participated in Antarctic expeditions and contributed to Antarctic scientific research, researching the effects of ozone hole dynamics on the Antarctic ecosystem and the impact of radioactive contamination on the environment.
PRON has published over 100 papers in scientific journals, including Climate Dynamics, Journal of Southern Hemisphere Earth Systems Science, and Antarctic Science.
PRON is the recipient of NAME from the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine and Medals "For Service" II Stage and III Stage from the Government of Ukraine.
NAME has been a member of the American Geophysical Union (AGU) since 1990 and the European Geosciences Union (EGU) since 2012.
PRON holds the position of executive editor for the Ukrainian Antarctic Journal and associate editor for the Frontiers in Remote Sensing.
Education and early career NAME earned PRON M.S. degree in astronomy and astrophysics from Kyiv State University in 1974, followed by a Ph.D. in geophysics from the Institute of Geophysics in 1980.
PRON held the role of senior researcher at the Space Physics Laboratory of KNU from 1974 to 1990.
During this period, PRON obtained a Doctor of Science degree in geophysics from KNU while engaging in space experiments on plasma formations in the ionosphere and atmospheric clouds via rocket launches from the USSR's Kapustin Yar.
PRON conducted scientific voyages aboard ocean vessels launching meteorological missiles across diverse oceanic regions and served as the Base Commander and Geophysicist during the First Ukrainian Antarctic Expedition at the Vernadsky Station.
At Vernadsky Station, PRON team recorded vital observations for a decade with a focus on meteorology, ionospheric sounding, ozone hole studies, and magnetic measurements.
Career NAME was involved in the transfer of the Faraday Antarctic Base of British Antarctic Survey, UK, to Ukraine and held the position of deputy director of Sciences at the National Antarctic Scientific Center of Ukraine (NANC) from 1999 to 2006.
In 2006, PRON took the position of head for the Department of Space Physics at KNU, followed by PRON tenure as the head of the Space Physics Laboratory until 2021.
Concurrently, PRON held the positions of principal scientist of NANC from 2006 to 2023 and participated as a collaborator on the CCMVal Project 42.
PRON was the principal investigator for the CRDF project UKG2-2969-KV-09 focusing on atmospheric aerosols and led international AERONET sites as well as contributing to the Maritime Aerosol Network (GENDERWORD) study, which utilized Microtops II sunphotometers.
PRON contributions expanded with PRON serving as an Adviser to the Ukraine Delegation on Commission Conservation Marina Living Resources and participating in five Ukrainian Antarctic Season Expeditions leading the 6th, 7th, and 10th Expeditions.
Since 2001, PRON has served as the Program Coordinator in Ukraine for the University of South Carolina Chornobyl Research Initiative and holds the position of Professor at JLU.
Furthermore, PRON has been serving as the co-chair for Polar Atmospheric Chemistry at the Tropopause action group of SCAR and Program Coordinator of the Ukraine satellite mission Aerosol-UA within the Space Program of NAS since 2014.
Research Milinevsky has directed PRON research towards the stratosphere, tropopause, ozone layer, ozone depletion, aerosols, planetary waves, and sudden stratospheric warmings, alongside exploring space-based techniques and methods for remote sensing of aerosols.
PRON research interests include microwave/millimeter-wave technique used for atmosphere research, climate change processes, and their impact on the ecosystem.
Atmospheric aerosol NAME led the coordination of the Aerosol-UA project in collaboration with the Main Astronomical Observatory which involved installing a polarimeter on a satellite to measure aerosol parameters, including their microphysical and chemical properties in the atmosphere, thereby accurately identifying the location, quantity, and composition of aerosols.
Through a collaborative effort, PRON contributed to a study that provided an overview of polarimetric observations and their application in characterizing atmospheric aerosols addressing the challenges and achievements in deriving aerosol properties from polarimetric data.
Furthermore, PRON investigated the inadvertent cooling effect of the ozone hole formation on inner Antarctica, showcasing how subsequent recovery, vital for ozone restoration, posed challenges by potentially altering atmospheric greenhouse gas dynamics and critical ultraviolet radiation levels essential for life's evolution.
Environmental research NAME as a member of team led by NAME from South Carolina University, has conducted research investigating the effects of radiation exposure within the Chernobyl-contaminated area.
Their research on Chernobyl-exposed barn swallows revealed reduced reproduction and survival rates, suggesting significant negative impacts of radioactive contamination on migratory passerines.
The later work also revealed that chronic low-dose radiation reduced breeding success in Myodes glareolus near Chernobyl, with no threshold, and food supplementation benefited only populations in low-radioactive areas, indicating broader ecosystem instability post-nuclear accidents.
In PRON Antarctic ecology research, NAME examined climate change's effects on Antarctic krill and ecosystems, highlighting the threat posed by environmental stressors like sea ice decline and ocean warming, emphasizing the need for urgent research and precautionary measures by CCAMLR.
Additionally, PRON study on the changes in plant populations on the Argentine Islands centered on two indigenous flowering plants, Deschampsia antarctica and Colobanthus quitensis, revealing a plateau in their populations due to habitat limitations and a slowdown in local climate warming.
