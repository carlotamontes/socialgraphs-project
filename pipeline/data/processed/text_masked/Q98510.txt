PRON field of expertise includes the study of tides and seismic vibrations.
In recognition of PRON outstanding contributions, the Zurn Peak (1515m) in Antarctica was named in PRON honor.
Education Zurn studied physics in Stuttgart, Germany and wrote PRON thesis at the "Institute of Metal Physics".
Career After graduation, PRON spent four years at the "University of California" (UCLA).
Antarctic research From 1971 to 1972, Zurn was Science Manager at the Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station in Antarctica.
PRON said of the station, "This spot at the bottom of the world is a good observation post for numerous reasons including altitude, high magnetic latitude, remoteness from human interference and the ocean."
Research in Germany NAME returned to Germany in 1974 and joined the newly established German Geophysical Society, where PRON worked at the Universities of Stuttgart and Karlsruhe.
In the 1980s, PRON collaborated with NAME on groundbreaking experiments testing Newton's law of universal gravitation.
In recognition of PRON exceptional contributions to geophysics, NAME was awarded the inaugural Rebeur-Paschwitz Prize by the German Geophysical Society in 2004.
