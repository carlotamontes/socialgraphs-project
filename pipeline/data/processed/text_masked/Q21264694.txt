NAME is an X-ray physicist and physicochemist.
PRON develops methods for time-resolved X-ray experiments to illuminate chemical molecular processes for example 'filming' chemical reactions in real time.
Biography  Techert studied chemistry from 1988 to 1993 at the University of Giessen.
PRON graduated in 1994.
PRON finished PRON doctorate at the Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry in Göttingen in 1997.
The title of PRON doctoral thesis was "Spectroscopy and charge separation.
Theoretical and experimental studies on pyrene derivatives".
Afterwards, PRON worked as a scientist at the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility (ESRF) in Grenoble, until PRON returned to the Max Planck Institute as a group leader in 2001.
PRON was the head of the NAME group from 2006 to 2012.
PRON has been a professor of physical chemistry since 2004.
Since 2008 PRON has been a lecturer at the International Research School for Molecular Biophysics at the Göttingen Research Campus.
In 2013, PRON became a professor at the University of Göttingen with a connection to the Helmholtz Association and the German Electron Synchrotron (DESY) in Hamburg.
PRON is a Leading Scientist in the group "Chemical Structural Dynamics" at DESY.

2007: NAME medal in the chemical industry  2013: Morino Lectureship   Research area  Techert conducts research in the field of physical chemistry.
PRON has specialized in time-resolved methods in X-ray physics, time-resolved structure determination and time-resolved spectroscopy.
At DESY PRON works with PRON research group on the structural dynamics of chemical systems, especially the development of time-resolved X-ray experiments, their application and optimization for the investigation of elementary chemical processes and structure-dynamic relationships in chemical reactions, ie the "filming" of chemical reactions in real time.
PRON research team succeeded in 2010 in using the radiation from a free-electron laser to investigate chemical reactions.
PRON worked on the experimental recording of hydrogen bonds as part of the Helmholtz working group.
In 2017 PRON research (with collaborators) using time-resolved x-ray methods, laid the foundation for a new type of solar cell.
