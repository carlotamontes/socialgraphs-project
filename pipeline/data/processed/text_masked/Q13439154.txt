NAME (; born August 4, 1957) is a South Korean physicist and director of the Center for Correlated Electron Systems (CCES) in the Institute for Basic Science (IBS) at Seoul National University (SNU).
PRON has published more than 400 papers and been cited 15,000 times.
PRON is a member of the Materials Research Society, Korean Optical Society, Korean Crystallographic Society, and Association of Asia Pacific Physical Societies and has been on several editorial boards for journals.
In 2017, PRON became president of the Korean Dielectrics Society.
In 2024, PRON became the 9th president of the Korea Institute for Advanced Study.
Education NAME graduated from Kyunggi High School in Seoul, South Korea, in 1976 and received PRON B.Sc. (
1982) in Physics from Seoul National University.
Supervised by Dr. NAME, PRON obtained PRON M.S. and Ph.D. in physics from Ohio State University in 1984 and 1986, respectively.
Career Noh conducted postdoctoral research in Dr. NAME group at Cornell University until 1989.
During that period, PRON studied the optical properties of high-Tc superconductors, especially copper oxide.
In August 1989, PRON joined the faculty of the Department of Physics at Seoul National University starting as an assistant professor before becoming an associate and later full professor.
PRON was the director of the Research Center for Oxide Electronics at SNU from 2000.
With funding from the Institute for Basic Science, PRON established the Center for Correlated Electron Systems at Seoul National University in 2012.
The research center searches for new emerging phenomena at the interfaces of strongly correlated electron systems, such as oxides.
PRON became an Endowed-Chair Professor at SNU in 2017.
In 2019, the Center for Correlated Electron Systems and the Institute for Solid State Physics (ISSP) of the University of Tokyo opened a joint research laboratory at Seoul National University.
Noh performs research in condensed matter physics.
PRON research has focused on transition metal oxides but has extended to other strongly correlated electron systems.
PRON research interests include the growth of oxide thin films and artificial heterostructures, emerging phenomena in oxide surfaces and interfaces, the metal-insulator transition and orbital physics in transition metal oxides, the optical properties of numerous solids, and the physics of oxide devices such as ferroelectric random-access memory (FeRAM), resistance RAM (RRAM), and spintronic devices.
PRON has worked on novel nanoscale physical phenomena, especially in informational devices such as FeRAMs and RRAMs.
PRON elucidated the physical mechanism of the fatigue problem in FeRAMs.
PRON also addressed the critical thickness of ultrathin films where they lose ferroelectricity.
Additionally, PRON sought to explain resistance-switching phenomena, which are the basis of RRAM.
Recently, PRON developed a new percolation model, called the "random circuit-breaker network model", which can explain reversible switching phenomena.
PRON also discovered a novel quantum state of the correlated electron system in iridates that has attracted the interest of many researchers.
PRON investigated the Mott physics of Sr2IrO4, which has a Mott insulator phase in the Jeff = 1/2 state.
In the Korean Physical Society (KPS), PRON has served as treasurer (FY 1999–2000), executive officer (FY 2003–04), and vice president (FY 2009–12).
PRON served as a council member trustee of the Association of Asia and Pacific Physical Societies from 2011 – 2013, is on the board of trustees at the Asia Pacific Center for Theoretical Physics, and the International Advisory Board of the Asian Meeting on Ferroelectricity.
In 2014, PRON became a member of the International Union of Physics and Applied Physics.
PRON has served on the editorial boards of several international journals, including Applied Physics Letters, and as editor-in-chief of Current Applied Physics.
