NAME (born 5 March 1978, in Tübingen) is an Italian-American nanophysicist who has been a professor of physics at the University of Basel (Switzerland) since 2009 and director of the Swiss Nanoscience Institute at the University of Basel since August 2022.
In addition, PRON was head of the Department of Physics at the University of Basel from September 2019 to August 2021 and then deputy head until August 2023.
From 2020 to 2022, NAME also served as co-director of the National Center of Competence in Research (NCCR) QSIT.
Early life and education  NAME, whose parents are from Italy, has both Italian and American citizenship.
After graduating from the Roxbury Latin School, PRON completed PRON bachelor's in physics at Harvard University in 2000 and PRON PhD at the University of California, Santa Barbara in 2005.
During PRON studies, PRON worked for NAME in the field of ultrafast optics and semiconductor spintronics, completing PRON dissertation on the topic of "Spin Interactions Between Conduction Electrons and Local Moments in Semiconductor Quantum Wells".
NAME  After receiving PRON doctorate, PRON began work as a postdoctoral fellow at the Center for Probing the Nanoscale, a joint research centre of Stanford University and IBM.
Until the end of 2008, PRON worked on high-sensitivity magnetic resonance force microscopy in Dan Rugar's laboratory at the IBM Almaden Research Center in San Jose, and PRON was appointed as an assistant professor in the Department of Physics at the University of Basel in summer of that year.
Awarded an ERC Starting Grant in 2013, PRON was promoted by the university to associate professor in 2014 and full professor in 2020.
Along with NAME, NAME is one of two "Argovia" professors of nanosciences at the University of Basel, who are funded by the Swiss Nanoscience Institute (SNI).
Since August 2022, NAME has led the SNI as its director.
NAME works primarily in the area of nanomechanics and its applications in sensor technology.
In this context, nanomechanical sensors are used to take highly sensitive measurements of force, spin and charges on matter.
The mechanical "top-down" devices that are traditionally found in the semiconductor industry are now joined by a new class of self-assembled "bottom-up" structures that offer huge potential for improving sensitivity.
