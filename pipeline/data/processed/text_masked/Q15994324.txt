NAME (born 7 June 1961) is a British physicist and chemist who works at the London Centre for Nanotechnology and the Department of Physics and Astronomy,  University College London.
PRON is known for PRON experimental discovery of spin ice with NAME and PRON calculation of a critical exponent observed in two-dimensional magnets with NAME.
A probability distribution for global quantities in complex systems, the "Bramwell-Holdsworth-Pinton (BHP) distribution", (to be implemented in Mathematica) is named after PRON.
In 2009, NAME's group was one of several to report experimental evidence of magnetic monopole excitations in spin ice.
PRON coined the term "magnetricity" to describe currents of these effective magnetic "monopoles" in condensed-matter systems.
NAME studied chemistry at Oxford University, obtaining PRON PhD in 1989.
PRON was a professor of physical chemistry at University College London from 2000-2009, before becoming a Professor in the Department of Physics and Astronomy.
Honours and distinctions NAME was awarded the 2010 Holweck Prize of the British Institute of Physics and the Société Française de Physique (SFP) for "pioneering new concepts in the experimental and theoretical study of spin systems".
PRON shared the 2012 Europhysics Prize of the European Physical Society Condensed Matter Division "for the prediction and experimental observation of magnetic monopoles in spin ice".
PRON is a Fellow of the Institute of Physics.
In 2010 PRON won the Times Higher Education research project of the year for "magnetricity",  and was named by The Times on their list of the 100 top UK scientists.
In 2019, NAME was awarded an honorary doctorate (FDhc) at Uppsala University in recognition of PRON work on spin ice, two-dimensional magnetism and longstanding collaboration with researchers at the Ångström Laboratory.
