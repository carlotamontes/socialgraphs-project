NAME (born 22 June 1961, in Pasadena, California) is an American theoretical particle physicist.
PRON is a professor in the SLAC Theory Group at the Stanford Linear Accelerator Center (SLAC) at Stanford University.
NAME received in 1982 PRON B.S. in physics and applied mathematics from Caltech and received in 1986 PRON doctorate from Princeton University.
As a postdoc PRON was at SLAC.
From 1987 PRON was assistant professor at Princeton University, from 1989 PRON was a Panofsky Fellow at the SLAC and in 1992 PRON became an associate professor and in 1998 a full professor at SLAC.
PRON has been a visiting professor at the École normale supérieure and the University of Cambridge (Clare Hall).
In 1987, Dixon gave a talk at the International Centre for Theoretical Physics in Trieste where PRON described a symmetry of supersymmetric string theories that would become known as mirror symmetry.
Starting in the 1990s Dixon developed, with NAME and others, new methods (generalized unitarity methods among others) for the calculation of Feynman diagrams in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and other NAME–Mills theories.
These new methods became more relevant with the requirements of the Large NAME calculations in the 2000s and also provided new insights into the divergences in the supergravity perturbation series.
In 2014, with NAME and NAME, NAME received the Sakurai Prize for "pathbreaking contributions to the calculation of perturbative scattering amplitudes, which led to a deeper understanding of quantum field theory and to powerful new tools for computing QCD processes."
PRON 1991 publication with NAME and Jan Louis has over 800 citations.
In 1995 NAME was elected a Fellow of the American Physical Society.
PRON was elected a member of the National Academy of Sciences in 2022.
