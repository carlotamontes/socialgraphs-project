Career  NAME is an expert on mathematical approaches to biological systems, genomics of cancer and infectious diseases.

PRON has applied quantitative approaches to modeling and understanding the dynamics of biological systems through the lens of genomics.
PRON has focused PRON research on the evolution of two of such biological systems: cancer and infectious diseases.
In particular, PRON has been working on the identification of driver mechanisms of evolutionary processes, characterization of key process dynamics to elucidate  interactions.
NAME is interested in understanding the evolution of infectious agents through the analysis of their genome, in particular RNA viruses like influenza and coronaviruses.
PRON work in this area includes elucidating the origin of the influenza A virus subtype H1N1.
Since joining Columbia in 2008 most of NAME's work has focused on cancer genomics approaches to understand tumor evolution and heterogeneity, mostly on hematological malignancies, brain tumors, and uncovering the role of non-coding mutations and splicing variants in cancer.
PRON work on hematological malignancies has led to the identification of driver alterations in hairy cell leukemia, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, chronic lymphocytic leukemia, splenic marginal zone lymphoma among others.
PRON has been working on identifying driver alterations in glioblastomas, longitudinal studies of brain tumor evolution under standard therapy and immunotherapies, uncovering the role of clonal heterogeneity in brain tumors, mapping the role of pharmacogenomics in precision oncology therapies.
PRON has also been involved in studying the role of non-coding RNA and splicing mutations in cancer.
PRON has been leading an active program to bring tools from computer science, physics and mathematics into the study of biological systems.
PRON has been working on the application of topological data analysis to large scale genomic data and transcriptomic single cell data.
PRON has recently developed foundational models to understand cell type specific transcriptional programs in human cells.

The books explores biology in the age of Big Data.
This book introduces the central ideas and techniques of topological data analysis and its specific applications to biology, including the evolution of viruses, bacteria and humans, genomics of cancer, and single cell characterization of developmental processes.
In 2020 NAME published Understanding Coronavirus in Cambridge University Press.
